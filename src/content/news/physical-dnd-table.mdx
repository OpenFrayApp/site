I mostly roll in OpenFray now. But I keep three full dice sets beside me, because sometimes I want to throw twenty dice onto the table and see the players’ reaction.

Sometimes I want to scare them. Sometimes I want a “wow.” A result on my screen gives me the number; a handful of dice landing in front of everyone makes the roll part of the scene.

That choice is part of how I use a combat console at a physical DnD table. I draw maps by hand, my players use different kinds of character sheets, and a tablet shows them the fight. I choose what to put in OpenFray and what to handle with the things already in front of us.

## A room becomes a battlefield

For a whole dungeon, I use a regular A4 sheet of paper and a pencil. I draw the rooms and passages there. When we need a combat map, we zoom into the relevant room.

I have four tiles that fit together like a puzzle. I draw that part of the dungeon on them with whiteboard markers. The larger map stays on paper, and the room gets the space it needs on the table.

The physical map is where I judge positions, distances, cover, and which creatures a spell catches. Once I have decided what happens, I record the relevant changes in the console.

That leaves me with two places to look during a turn. The map shows the battlefield. OpenFray holds turn order, hit points, and the effects I have recorded. If a creature is affected by a spell, that effect stays beside its name when I come back to it.

Supported spells can apply their tracked effects to the targets I choose. I add other effects or reminders myself. The [article on tracking effects](/news/what-your-initiative-tracker-forgets/) shows how Bane, Faerie Fire, and Prone reach the right rolls.

## The screen faces both ways

My wife gave me a [Dragon Shield Game Master Companion](https://www.dragonshield.com/en-eu/collections/game-master-companion) (not a sponsor), which I use to carry everything and as my GM screen. Behind it, I keep sheets of random names grouped by race and suited to my homebrew world. When I need a name during play, I reach for a sheet.

In front of the screen, a tablet faces the players with OpenFray’s player view open. They can see the turn order, effect badges, and game log. I have the controls and creature details on my side.

Some players use pen and paper with physical dice. Others use D&D Beyond and make digital rolls there. Their sheets hold their characters’ abilities; the tablet gives everyone a shared view of the fight. They can leave their phones on their character sheets or keep their paper sheets beside them.

I still have to record what happens. OpenFray does not read a physical die or receive a roll from D&D Beyond. The handoff looks like this:

| At the table                                  | In the console                                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| A player rolls physical dice or in D&D Beyond | I enter the result or apply the resulting change |
| I decide who a spell catches on the map       | I choose the targets and record the effects      |
| An action ends a condition                    | I remove the recorded condition                  |

The players’ screen follows the changes I make. I wrote more about [how that tablet helped my fights](/news/in-person-dnd-player-screen/), including what I show and keep private.

## When I want everyone to see the roll

For most rolls on my side, I use the console. I can roll from a creature’s stat block, recorded effects can contribute to supported rolls, and the calculation stays in the log for me to check afterward.

Physical dice come out for particular reasons. Sometimes the important thing is that everyone witnesses the result. Other times, I want the sound and sight of that large handful landing on the table.

> “I roll physical when I want it to have an effect.”

A small die has its uses too. I might roll a d4, d6, or d8 to set an order or choose a direction. I also have a coin with Yes and No on its two faces. When I want the players to leave a decision to chance, I hand it over.

When a physical result matters to the tracked fight, I record it in OpenFray afterward. I can choose how to make each roll and still keep the changes together on the board.

Between sessions, our WhatsApp group holds the images and lasting information I want the players to find again. We use it to organize the next game too. The console is where I keep the changing state of a fight and my private campaign and character notes. The group is where we return to shared information between games.
