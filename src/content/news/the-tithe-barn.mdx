Every creature here comes from [The Waking Garden](/the-waking-garden/), so the whole
adventure is already in the console — turn the library on and add them by name. Three
fights, 1,550 XP in total, with room for a short rest between the second and the third.

## The situation

A village keeps its winter store in a stone barn at the end of a walled lane. Eight days
ago the crop inside woke. Six days ago the barn was barred from the inside, which nobody
can account for, because the only door bars from the inside and there is nobody in there
to have done it.

Four days ago a crow began talking to the village in the reeve's own voice. It wants a
tithe. It has already taken the barn key, and it has taken other things since, and it
keeps all of it somewhere the villagers cannot reach.

The party is hired to get the key back. The key opens the barn; the barn holds the
winter; the winter is the whole of the problem. Nobody in the village has said out loud
what happens if the party fails, and the reeve will not be drawn on it.

## Scene 1: The lane

**Levels 2–3 · 200 XP**

A walled lane 15 feet wide and 200 feet long, running between the village and the barn
gate. Asparagus was bedded along both walls last spring and has come up in rank. There
is no way around it: the walls are chest-high field stone, and going over them puts a
creature in the beds, which is where the rows are.

The [Speartip Rows](/the-waking-garden/chapter-2/#c-speartip-row) cannot chase and do
not need to. Each has a reach of 15 feet, which is the width of the lane, and each
braces against anything that comes at it straight. Standing within 10 feet of one
another they close rank and are harder to hit, so the lane is worth clearing from one
end rather than pushing through the middle.

The two [Weeping Onions](/the-waking-garden/chapter-2/#c-weeping-onion) are the reason
the fight is not simply a matter of reach. Anything that ends up in the beds is inside
their aura, and the party's ranged answer to the rows stops working well from there.

| Creature      |  №  | CR  | Placement                                                      |
| ------------- | :-: | :-: | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Speartip Row  |  3  | 1/4 | Two in the east bed within 10 feet of each other, one opposite |
| Weeping Onion |  2  | 1/8 | In the beds behind the rows, level with the second row         |

**Running it.** The lane exists to make the party choose a side and commit to it. A
creature that spends its turn crossing to the other bed spends it inside two auras and
inside two reaches. Nothing here pursues, so a party that withdraws to the village end
takes no further attacks — the encounter ends whenever they decide it has, and the barn
is still barred.

## Scene 2: The threshing floor

**Levels 2–3 · 750 XP**

The barn is 60 feet by 30 feet, earth-floored, with a threshing floor down the middle
and the season's pumpkins stacked four deep along both long walls. The great door is
barred. A ladder at the north end goes up to the seed loft through a hatch in the
ceiling, and the hatch is the only other way out of the room.

Four of the pumpkins are [Grinning Gourds](/the-waking-garden/chapter-2/#c-grinning-gourd),
and until one of them lights up there is no telling which. Their reach pulls a creature
off the threshing floor and into the stacks; the stacks are where the rest of them are.
Two [Snaproots](/the-waking-garden/chapter-2/#c-snaproot) are under the earth floor
itself, so the open middle of the room is not the safe ground it looks like.

The [Tithe-Crow](/the-waking-garden/chapter-6/#c-tithe-crow) is on a rafter, and it
speaks first. It will name a price, in the reeve's voice or in the voice of whoever in
the party it has heard longest. It does not fight to kill, and it leaves the moment it
has taken something from someone or drops below half its Hit Points — through the hatch,
every time, because that is where its hoard is.

| Creature       |  №  | CR  | Placement                                             |
| -------------- | :-: | :-: | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Tithe-Crow     |  1  |  2  | On the rafters, 20 feet up, above the threshing floor |
| Grinning Gourd |  4  | 1/4 | Two per wall, second row back in the stacks           |
| Snaproot       |  2  | 1/4 | Under the threshing floor, mid-room                   |

**Running it.** The crow is not the boss of this room and does not have the endurance to
be one. It is a timer: everything it does is aimed at leaving with one more thing than it
came with, and the room's real question is whether the party can reach the ladder or the
hatch before it goes up. A party that ignores the gourds to chase the crow gets pulled
into the stacks; a party that clears the stacks first arrives in the loft later and
poorer.

The key is not on the crow. It is in the hoard.

## Scene 3: The seed loft

**Levels 2–3 · 600 XP**

The loft runs the length of the barn under the roof, 60 feet by 15 feet, floored in
board, and it is half full of seed sacks stacked to head height. Two dormers let in
enough light to see by. The floor is thick with spilled seed, and everything in it has
been germinating for eight days.

A [Bindweed Creeper](/the-waking-garden/chapter-5/#c-bindweed-creeper) has come up
through the boards and along the rafters, which is where its 10-foot reach is worth
having. It comes back from any scrap of root left in the floor unless it is burned or
the root is dug out, so a party that cuts it down and moves on will be fighting it again
a minute later, from behind.

Two [Podswarms](/the-waking-garden/chapter-2/#c-podswarm) are in the sacks and pour out
of them. A [Runt Patch](/the-waking-garden/chapter-2/#c-runt-patch) is in the spilled
seed at the far end, between the party and the hoard, and by this point in the session it
will have someone below half their Hit Point maximum to prefer.

The hoard is in the north dormer: the barn key, a signet, three knives, a component
pouch, and forty feet of good rope, none of it damaged. The crow took what it wanted and
kept it well.

| Creature         |  №  | CR  | Placement                                               |
| ---------------- | :-: | :-: | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bindweed Creeper |  1  |  1  | Along the rafters over the middle of the loft           |
| Runt Patch       |  1  |  1  | In the spilled seed at the north end, before the dormer |
| Podswarm         |  2  | 1/2 | Inside two sacks, one on each side of the aisle         |

**Running it.** The loft is a corridor with swarms in it, and the swarms make their own
Difficult Terrain, so the aisle closes as the fight goes on. The Creeper's regrowth is the
part that decides whether the scene has an end: it is meant to be dealt with rather than
outlasted, and a party carrying Fire or Radiant damage will find this loft much shorter
than one that is not.

If the crow is still alive it is on the roof beam, out of reach, keeping up a commentary
in a borrowed voice. It has nothing left to take and does not join in.

## If the crow gets away

It usually does, and the adventure does not depend on it dying. It has the party's voices
now, and it knows the way to the village. What it does with a second season of tithe is a
thread to pick up later, and the barn is open either way.

## Running it in the console

Everything above resolves in [the console](/console/) without leaving the tab:

1. Open **Settings** — the gear at the top right — and tick **The Waking Garden** under
   OpenFray's own libraries.
2. Click **Add creature** and type a few letters of each name. Adding the same creature
   twice names the second one _Grinning Gourd 2_, so the four in the stacks stay apart on
   the tracker.
3. Add your players with **Add PC**, or pick them from your saved characters if you are
   signed in.
4. Click **Begin** to roll initiative for the room.

Four things in this adventure are worth setting up as effects rather than remembering:

- **The Weeping Aura.** OpenFray does not run an aura on its own. Put a reminder on each
  character standing in one so the disadvantage is in front of you when they roll.
- **Ember Heart.** A gourd's parting explosion does not fire by itself. **Group save**
  resolves it against everyone nearby in one step.
- **Rampant Regrowth.** Apply an effect with **Duration → 10 rounds** and a reminder
  naming the Creeper, so the regrowth arrives on a turn rather than when someone
  remembers it.
- **One for the Hedge.** A reminder on the crow reading _leaves at 22 hit points_ puts
  its exit next to its hit points, where you will see it.

The [handbook page on effects](/docs/fight/effects/) covers all four in more detail, and
[group saves](/docs/fight/saves/) covers the second.
