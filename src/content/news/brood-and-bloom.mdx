[Brood & Bloom](/brood-and-bloom/) is a bestiary of sixty-one creatures whose lives run
through other bodies, living, dead or built. Like [The Waking
Garden](/the-waking-garden/), it is published here in full and free to read, and it is
built into the console.

The thing that makes it different to run is that a lot of it happens after the fight ends.

## Three broods that want different things

The creatures are in three broods. They are unrelated, and the distinction is practical
rather than taxonomic — it is about where the party meets them.

| Brood          | Chapter | What it is                                                             |
| -------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Inquiline**  | 2       | Infects one person. What a party carries away                          |
| **Sporophore** | 3       | Infects ground and buildings. What a party walks into                  |
| **Necrophore** | 4       | Wants nothing from the living. What a party leaves behind, in its dead |

Each runs on a counter the console can hold for you. The Inquiline tracks **Depth**, which
never recedes on its own — the graft has to come out. The Sporophore tracks **Spore Load**,
which washes off with rest and distance, because what has to be left is the colony rather
than the passenger. The Necrophore has neither, and that is the point of it: what it asks
of a party is what they do with their dead.

## Read chapter 1 first

This is the one instruction the library gives, and it is worth repeating here. Several of
these creatures do most of their work between sessions, and their stat blocks lean on the
rules around them: the classifications, the counters, what happens at the last stage of a
disease, and which spells help — a shorter list than you would expect, and one of them
makes a particular case worse.

That framing chapter is also where the order lives. The Lazaret has been writing this down
for longer than anyone remembers, and its three ranks are statted so you can put one at the
table: a **Registrar** at CR 1, a **Prosector** at CR 4, and a **Lector** at CR 5. Rank
follows the career, so the officer running a house is the strongest of the three rather
than the weakest.

## From nothing to an apex

Every brood runs a full ladder. The Inquiline opens on a CR 0 latchling and ends on the
**Exemplar** at CR 17; the Sporophore runs from a driftling to the **Nave**; the Necrophore
from a cinder nit to the **Lychfield**. An appendix lays all three life cycles out
together — what grows into what, what settles where, and what lays in what — so you can
see where a line is going before you commit a campaign to it.

## Using it at the table

Open **Settings** in [the console](/console/) and tick **Brood & Bloom**. Its creatures
join **Add creature** with everything else.

The [counters](/docs/fight/effects/#counters) are worth setting up as you go. Select the
character, click **Apply effect**, set **Duration** to **Counter**, and type `Depth` or
`Spore Load` into **Reminder**. It starts at 0 and sits on their row as a badge with the
number in it, with **+1**, **−1** and **Reset** beside it in the controls.

OpenFray never moves a counter on its own — you do, and you decide what it means when it
gets high. That is exactly how this library wants to be run, and it is why the counters
are counters rather than something the app resolves for you.
