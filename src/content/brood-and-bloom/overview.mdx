_A bestiary of parasites, compatible with 5.5e and 5e_

Sixty-seven creatures whose lives run through other bodies — living, dead, or built —
with the rules for what they leave behind in the people, the ground, and the buildings
that survive them, and the order of physicians that has been writing all of it down for
longer than anyone can remember.

Every stat block is compatible with 5.5e and 5e. It is tuned mainly for the power level
of 5.5e, so some creatures may feel stronger than their listed challenge rating in a 5e
game.

The creatures are arranged in three broods. The broods are not related to one another
and do not want the same things:

- **[Inquiline](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-4/)** infects one person at a time. It is what a party carries away.
- **[Sporophore](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-5/)** infects ground rather than people. It is what a party walks into.
- **[Necrophore](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-6/)** wants nothing from the living. It is what a party leaves behind, in its dead.

**[Chapter 3](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-3/)** is the people. It covers the cult that welcomes
all three broods. **[Chapter 7](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-7/)** holds the alchemy and the
spells the order, the cult, and lone practitioners keep to themselves.

Read **[chapters 1](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-1/)** and
**[2](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-2/)** first. Several of these creatures do most of their work
after the fight ends, and their stat blocks depend on the rules around them — the Lazaret's
classifications, the counters each brood runs on, and what magic does and does not do
here.

The appendices offer three ways to find what you need: **[by Challenge Rating](/brood-and-bloom/appendix-a/)**,
**[by type](/brood-and-bloom/appendix-b/)**, and **[by alchemy or magic](/brood-and-bloom/appendix-c/)**.

Use as much of it as you want. One creature is an encounter. One line is an adventure.
A brood followed to its apex is a campaign arc.
