The Lazaret classes these parasites as inquilines, from the old word for a thing that
lives inside the dwelling of another. Common folk call them latchkin. An inquiline
feeds on living bodies one at a time, and what it leaves behind travels with the
victim — this brood is the one a party carries away. The Lazaret's standing instruction
for it: **the parasite first, then the graft.**

## How an inquiline works

An inquiline implants. It latches onto a living creature, feeds every round it can —
each feeding adds 1 Depth — and stays until it is pulled off or has taken all it can
hold. Its first feeding roots a **graft** in the host, and parasite and graft then
grow together: the parasite on the body, riding it and feeding, and the graft inside
it, beyond the reach of anything but surgery. Growth happens nowhere else. A latchling
that keeps its host for days becomes its line's next form without ever letting go, and
the only inquiline that leaves a host willingly is a sated adult, off to lay.

That is what extraction is for, and why the brood is built to resist it: an attached
inquiline moves with its host (Embedded), relocates against every attempt to remove it
(Shift Anchor), and an attack that nearly misses it wounds the host instead. An
attached inquiline is not trying to win the fight — every round attached is a meal,
and it profits from any fight that lasts.

Each inquiline's **Extraction** trait covers pulling the creature itself off a host.
Extraction stops the feeding; it never undoes it. The graft is a separate problem, and
most of this chapter is about it.

## The graft

A graft is a short length of the parasite's own tissue, left rooted in the host by its
first feeding. It is a cutting in the gardener's sense: an independent copy of its
parent, already growing, and alive only while it has a body to grow in. Killing or
removing the parent does not affect it.

### What it looks like

Pale and threadlike at first, an inch or two long, and easy to
mistake for a vein or a healed cut. It thickens as it grows; by stage 3 it stands up
under the skin as a raised line, and anyone who has seen one before knows it on sight.

### Which line it belongs to

A graft belongs to its parent's line. A graft left by a
latchling — which has no line yet — belongs to the line the host's own body sets: a
physical score (Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution) highest makes it sallow,
Intelligence makes it chantry, Wisdom or Charisma makes it palimpsest. Each line's
graft differs:

| Line       | The graft                                 | Where it roots                                         |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sallow     | Wet, pale, and soft, like a length of gut | Along the lymph, toward the deep muscle                |
| Chantry    | Fine and black, and hard as wire          | Along the nerves, toward wherever the host keeps magic |
| Palimpsest | Almost transparent, and warm to the touch | Up the spine, toward the base of the skull             |

### Where it is

A sallow or chantry graft has a fixed location. Roll 1d6 or choose:

| 1d6 | Location     |
| --- | ------------ |
| 1-2 | Arm          |
| 3-4 | Leg          |
| 5   | Torso        |
| 6   | Head or neck |

A palimpsest graft always roots in the spine, which is why no blade short of the Lazaret's reaches one.

## Depth

**Depth** measures how far a graft has worked into a host. It runs from 0 to 6, and it
is one of the two counters in this library — the Sporophore's Spore Load in
[chapter 3](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-3/) runs the same 0-to-6 shape.

### Gaining it

A host gains 1 Depth whenever an inquiline's feeding action resolves
against it, on a successful saving throw as well as a failed one — the save reduces
what the feeding takes, never what it leaves. If the host carries no graft from that
line, the feeding also leaves one. A host also gains 1 Depth at the end of every Long
Rest taken while it carries a graft: the graft works inward while the body is still.

### One graft per line

A host carries at most one graft from each line. Further
feedings from that line deepen the graft already there — the 1d4 + 1 latchlings a
clutch fastens to one character all feed the same graft. Grafts from different lines
act and progress independently, and their effects stack.

**At 6 Depth** the graft deepens. The host contracts the line's disease at stage 1, or
advances that disease one stage if it already has it, and Depth resets to 0.

<Note>
  **Depth never recedes on its own.** Rest does not remove it and distance does not either; the
  graft has to come out. The treatments below remove it.
</Note>

## Diagnosis

A graft announces nothing. Until its disease presents, the only ways to find one are
magic and a trained eye:

- **Detect Poison and Disease** finds every graft in range and names its line.
- **Examination.** A creature that spends 10 minutes examining a patient makes a DC 13
  Wisdom (Medicine) check — DC 16 once a disease is at stage 1 or higher. **Success:**
  The examiner finds the graft and identifies its line by its look and course (see
  [The graft](#the-graft)). **Failure:** The examiner finds nothing, and further
  examinations of that patient find nothing until the disease advances a stage.

Knowing what to do with the find is training, not the check. The Expulsion processes
are Lazaret teaching, published with the rest of its files: a creature proficient in
Medicine learned them along with its trade, wherever the order's copies reach. An
examiner without that training can find and describe a graft and not know what drives
it out — a lazaret house, an officer, or a file supplies the process.

Identifying the line is the point of diagnosis. The treatments below work on every
line, but Expulsion differs by line, and a patient sweated for 6 hours over a chantry
graft has suffered for nothing.

## Treatment

The Lazaret treats a graft in four tiers. Care slows it, Expulsion drives it out,
surgery and amputation cut it out, and magic works it loose a little at a time:

1. **Care.** A creature that spends 4 hours of downtime tending the patient makes a DC 15
   Wisdom (Medicine) check. **Success:** The graft stills, and the patient gains no
   Depth from the next Long Rest it finishes. Spending at least 6 hours tending the patient grants Advantage on the check.
2. **Expulsion.** A downtime procedure, different per line, that makes the body unfit
   to live in (see below). It removes the graft outright, at any stage and any Depth.
3. **Surgery or amputation.** Both remove the graft outright, at any stage and any
   Depth (see below). Neither reaches a palimpsest graft — only the Lazaret's officers
   have the knowledge and skills to take one from the spine.
4. **Magic.** The restoration spells remove Depth, walking the disease back a stage (see below).

However the graft comes out, its disease ends within 1d4 hours of the removal.

### Expulsion

Expulsion removes a graft by denying it, for as long as the line takes to give up,
whatever that line cannot live without. A completed Expulsion removes the graft
entirely — sweated out through the skin, passed, or brought up — at whatever stage and
Depth it had reached:

| Line       | Process                                                                                                                                                           | Why it works                                                   | Cost                                                                                 |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sallow     | 6 continuous hours in extreme dry heat — a sweat house, a forge floor at full fire, bare rock in high summer                                                      | The graft is water-bound and cannot follow the moisture out    | 3 Exhaustion levels                                                                  |
| Chantry    | 24 continuous hours with no unexpended spell slot, no magical feature, no spell or magical effect targeting the host, and no magical aura or location around them | The graft feeds on magic in the body and finds none            | 1 Exhaustion level, and a full day of recovery after                                 |
| Palimpsest | 24 continuous hours spent insensible — heavily drugged, comatose, or deprived of every sense                                                                      | There is nothing coherent to read, so there is nothing to take | 3 Exhaustion levels, a full day of recovery after, and the cost of the insensibility |

Expulsion removes the graft, not the parasite. A host who spends 6 hours in a sweat
house with a fluke still attached walks out clean and is grafted again at the next
feeding — extraction comes first, always.

### Surgery and amputation

Two procedures take a graft out through the skin:

- **Surgery** takes 30 minutes per stage of the disease (30 minutes if none has
  presented), a set of tools, and a Wisdom (Medicine) check against a Difficulty Class
  of 10 + the disease's stage + the graft's Depth, made by someone other than the
  patient. A trained physician makes the check with Advantage; a Lazaret officer
  automatically succeeds on the check. **Success:** The graft comes out whole, and the patient takes
  1d8 Slashing damage per stage of the disease (minimum 1d8). **Failure:** The graft
  stays, the patient takes the damage anyway, and further attempts on the same graft
  are made with Disadvantage for the next 3 days.
- **Amputation.** If the graft is in an arm or a leg, taking the limb takes the graft,
  with no check. It needs a cutting tool and fire. The patient takes 33 (6d10)
  Slashing damage and 9 (2d8) Fire damage, gains 4 Exhaustion levels, and has lost the
  limb. A bone saw and a practiced hand reduce the damage to 11 (3d6) Slashing and the
  Exhaustion to 2 levels.

Neither procedure reaches a palimpsest graft. It roots in the spine, past any field
surgery, and for that line the routes are Expulsion, magic, and the Lazaret: spinal
excision is the surgery the order's officers perform with Excise (see
[the officers](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-1/#officers)), and they are the only surgeons
who do it.

Extraction removes the parasite, not the graft. A party that pulls a cyst off a
friend, binds the wound, and moves on has treated the visible half of the problem.

### Magic

A restoration spell makes the body briefly reject tissue that is not its own, and
what it does depends on how deeply that tissue is anchored. A parasite grips the
surface, and the rejection throws it off whole — each stat block's Extraction says
which spell is enough. A graft is woven into lymph, nerve, or spine, and can only be
worked loose: the spell removes Depth, and the deeper the graft sits, the less each
casting moves it. One casting does both at once, detaching the parasite and removing
Depth:

| Spell               | Depth removed                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Lesser Restoration  | 1d4, and only below stage 3          |
| Greater Restoration | 2d4 below stage 3, or 1d4 at stage 3 |
| Heal                | 3d4 below stage 3, or 2d4 at stage 3 |
| Mass Heal           | As Heal, for every creature it heals |

<Note>
  **The counter runs both ways.** Removing more Depth than a host has does not stop at zero: the
  graft slips back a stage, and the host lands part-way up the stage below — 6 Depth given back for
  every stage lost. A host at stage 3 with 2 Depth whose Heal spell rolls a 6 ends at stage 2 with 4
  Depth.
</Note>

**Stage 1 with 0 Depth is the floor, and reaching it removes the graft.** A caster has
to walk the graft down every rung it climbed:

| Where the host is | Depth a caster must remove |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| Stage 1, Depth 3  | 3                          |
| Stage 2, Depth 3  | 9                          |
| Stage 3, Depth 3  | 15                         |
| Stage 3, Depth 5  | 17                         |

Expulsion, surgery, and amputation skip all of this; magic is the only treatment that
works a rung at a time. Stage 4 is beyond all four tiers — see
[Stage 4](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-1/#stage-4) and each disease below.

## Death and the graft

A graft eats what its host provides — flesh, magic, or memory — and two of those die
with the body.

A host that dies while carrying a chantry or palimpsest graft starves
it: the graft lives 1d4 days on what remains, then dies in place, and the corpse is
clean. A creature raised inside that window comes back still carrying the graft, at
the Depth it had; a creature raised afterward comes back clean.

The sallow line is the exception. A sallow graft feeds on flesh, and death does not
take the flesh anywhere: it stays, it keeps eating, and what comes out of the body at
the end is a [Quagdam](#c-quagdam) rather than a corpse. That is why only one of the
three diseases kills — for the sallow line, the host's death is the last step of
feeding.

## Laying grounds

Every sated adult leaves its host to lay, and each line lays where its food will pass.
The ground an adult chooses is where a clutch waits, where the first feedings happen,
and where the [lancebills](#c-lancebill) stand:

| Line       | Where it lays                                                                       | What the ground looks like                                                              |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sallow     | Still fresh water — fen pools, flooded fields, the warm mud below a ford            | Clean-picked frogs and fish along the margin, and wading birds that never leave         |
| Chantry    | Quiet, spell-soaked places — libraries, shrines, reliquaries, a wizard's cold rooms | Nothing. A chantry ground is indistinguishable from the room it was                     |
| Palimpsest | Where the dead are kept and the living come to remember — crypts, ossuaries         | Mourners who linger too long, and caretakers who have begun to misremember small things |

Latchlings die fast away from their ground.

## The diseases

Each line's graft causes one disease, and every one of them ends the same way: the
graft finishes, and something leaves the body. What differs is what it leaves behind:

| Line       | The Lazaret's name | Common name    | At stage 4                                                                 |
| ---------- | ------------------ | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sallow     | Sallow Rot         | the wetting    | The host dies. The graft stays in the corpse and matures into a Quagdam    |
| Chantry    | Chantry Drought    | the emptying   | The graft emerges as a Psalter Tick. The host lives on as a Lacuna         |
| Palimpsest | —                  | the Forgetting | The graft emerges as a Palimpsest Wyrm. The host lives on as an Amanuensis |

A graft that reaches stage 4 has eaten a whole person's worth of what it feeds on —
flesh, magic, or memory — and skips its line's small forms accordingly. The
[Lacuna](#c-lacuna) and the [Amanuensis](#c-amanuensis) are not the disease; they are
what is left when the disease is done.

### Sallow Rot

The Lazaret files it as Sallow Rot; common folk call it the wetting. A creature
contracts it at stage 1 when it reaches 6 Depth from a sallow graft:

| Stage | Effect                                                                                         |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | The creature's Speed decreases by 5 feet, and it gains no benefit from a Short Rest.           |
| 2     | The creature's Hit Point maximum decreases by 10, and its Speed decreases by 10 feet.          |
| 3     | The creature's Hit Point maximum decreases by a further 20, and healing it receives is halved. |
| 4     | The creature dies. The graft stays with the body and matures, 1d4 days later, into a Quagdam.  |

Hit Point maximum reductions last until the disease ends and do not stack with
themselves — a creature at stage 3 is down 30, not 30 on top of 10.

<Note type="design note">
  Sallow Rot is the plainest disease in the library and usually the first a party meets, at low
  levels, from a cyst picked up in a fen. It teaches the whole system in miniature: something got
  in, it fed, and days later somebody is slower and does not heal right.
</Note>

### Chantry Drought

The Lazaret files it as Chantry Drought; common folk call it the emptying. The graft
feeds on the magic in the host's body, and the body loses the ability to hold magic as
the graft advances and feeds on it. A creature contracts it at stage 1 when it reaches 6 Depth from a
chantry graft.

While a creature carries a chantry graft, **it gains 1 Depth whenever it expends a spell slot** of level 1 or higher.

**Restoration magic adds Depth.** A Lesser Restoration, Greater Restoration,
Heal, or Mass Heal spell cast on a creature carrying a chantry graft adds 1d4
Depth instead of removing any. The graft consumes the spell's magic like any other
magic in the body. Of the five spells in
[What magic does](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-1/#what-magic-does), only Detect Poison and Disease is safe to cast on a chantry case — identify the line before treating it. A
Lesser Restoration spell still detaches a chantry parasite, as each stat block's
Extraction says, but what would have worked the graft loose feeds it instead: the
parasite drops off well fed, and the graft deepens.

| Stage | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Casting a spell of level 1 or higher costs the creature 1d6 Necrotic damage per level of the spell. This damage can't be reduced, but Immunity to Necrotic damage prevents it.                                                                                   |
| 2     | One of the creature's highest-level spell slots can't be recovered on a Long Rest.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 3     | The creature's two highest-level spell slots can't be recovered on a Long Rest, and it has Disadvantage on Constitution saving throws made to maintain Concentration.                                                                                            |
| 4     | The creature loses its spellcasting entirely and has the Incapacitated and Unconscious conditions. It does not die. Over 1d4 days the graft withdraws along the nerves and emerges, swollen with a lifetime of magic, as a Psalter Tick. What wakes is a Lacuna. |

**Design note.** Chantry Drought is the only disease in this library a victim can
worsen by acting: each leveled spell the host casts moves it closer to stage 4. Stage 4
does not kill. The host survives as a Lacuna, the graft emerges as a Psalter Tick after
1d4 days, and both stat blocks are in this chapter.

### The Forgetting

The only disease in this library the Lazaret has never formally named: the first three
registrars assigned to write the file each left a different account of what it does,
and the common name stood by default. A creature contracts it at stage 1 when it
reaches 6 Depth from a palimpsest graft:

| Stage | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | The creature loses one language, one tool proficiency, or one skill proficiency of the Game Master's choice. It does not know it lost it; it forgets it ever had it.                                                        |
| 2     | One of the creature's mental ability scores (Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma) decreases by 2, and it gains a proficiency belonging to a mind the graft's line has eaten in the past.                                      |
| 3     | A second mental ability score decreases by 2. The creature can no longer reliably distinguish its own memories from the ones it has been given.                                                                             |
| 4     | The creature does not die. Over 1d4 days the graft uncoils from the spine and emerges, grown fat on a whole life's recollection, as a Palimpsest Wyrm. What remains — and insists that nothing happened — is an Amanuensis. |

**Design note.** The Forgetting takes things from a character without telling the
player: stage 1 is written to be tracked silently, and noticed at the table only when
the character reaches for what is gone. The stage 2 gain is the counterweight and is
meant to be played straight — the character is genuinely better at something they were
never taught, and cannot say where it came from.

## The creatures

### The shared larva

Every inquiline begins as a latchling, and a clutch is what a laying adult leaves
behind:

<Creature name="Latchling">
  **Running it.** The latchling exists to Feed, not to fight: its attack deals 1 damage, and
  everything it takes goes through Feed. Left attached, it feeds, deepens its graft, and after 1d4 +
  2 days is a cyst, a louse, or a worm — the encounter ends quickly; what it leaves does not. A
  single latchling threatens nothing; in groups, Stealth +7 still lands attachments, which is all
  the encounter requires.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Latchling Clutch">
  **Running it.** The clutch exists to place latchlings; its damage is low for its Challenge Rating
  because latchlings are its output. Everywhere at Once means a creature in its space carries at
  least one latchling out of the encounter even on a successful save. Each latchling then needs its
  own Extraction check, or one Lesser Restoration spell clears every latchling on one creature.
  Disperse is unavailable after the clutch takes Fire damage, so fire is the one way to hold it in
  place.
</Creature>

### The sallow line

The sallow line holds the wetlands — fens, flooded fields, fords — and it is the only
line in the brood that can use a corpse. A latchling that
feeds on a strong, quick, or hardy host becomes a mudspit cyst, then a sallow fluke,
then something that no longer needs to ride anybody:

<Creature name="Mudspit Cyst">
  **Running it.** Sap accumulates: each failed save costs the host 5 feet of Speed, the reductions
  hold until a rest with no cyst attached, and a host that keeps failing ends the fight Prone and
  unable to stand. Water-Bound bounds where a cyst appears — away from fresh water it has one hour
  to find a host or die, which is why a lone cyst in dry country attacks at once.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Sallow Fluke">
  **Running it.** Weapon attacks answer an embedded fluke poorly: it resists all three physical
  damage types, and Embedded's near-miss clause sends failed attacks into the host. Extraction is
  the built-in solution and Shift Anchor its counter. Deepen is the first effect in the library that
  can kill a host outright, and its Hit Point maximum reduction survives the fight until a Long Rest
  taken without the fluke.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Mire Ambulant">
  **Running it.** An ambulant no longer needs a host and behaves accordingly: it fights at reach,
  withdraws into water when hurt, and attaches only to a target already down or held. It resists the
  three physical damage types and swims at 40 feet, so a party without a way to hold it in place
  fights it more than once.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Quagdam">
  **Running it.** The quagdam cannot leave the water and never has to: Draw Down reaches 30 feet,
  heals it for half of what it takes, and is the brood's only feeding that works at range — it
  grafts a party rather than a person. Gravid means killing it does not end the encounter; a
  Latchling Clutch emerges when it drops to 0 Hit Points.
</Creature>

### The chantry line

The chantry line grows on casters, and every stage after the first is harder,
quieter, and better at hiding what it is doing:

<Creature name="Chantry Louse">
  **Running it.** The louse taxes one caster three ways: Siphon takes a spell slot, Backwash
  punishes casting, and Arcane Tropism rewards attacking whoever just cast. Siphon takes the lowest
  unexpended slot first, so the exchange runs for several rounds rather than ending on one save. A
  Lesser Restoration spell detaches it — the louse drops off unhurt and well fed, and the graft it
  left is already inside.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Psalter Tick">
  **Running it.** Stolen Slots starts empty: everything the tick can do beyond biting has to be
  taken from the party first, which makes the opening round the cheapest one. A taken slot can come
  back as Counterspell or Dispel Magic, and Misty Step is on the list so a sated tick can leave.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Choirgall">
  **Running it.** The choirgall is fed by casting near it: Sympathetic Resonance stacks across a
  round, and Antiphon and Unmake are each once a day, so its early rounds are Chime and patience. It
  is the one inquiline that never attaches — its host is already spent — so nothing in this fight is
  about pulling something off a friend.
</Creature>

### The palimpsest line

The palimpsest line feeds on memory. It grows slowest, roots deepest, and is the only
line whose graft no blade outside the Lazaret reaches:

<Creature name="Gaol Worm">
  **Running it.** Convulse redirects the party's damage into the host: every hit on an attached worm
  costs the host a saving throw. Sleepless persists between encounters — the host gains no benefit
  from a Long Rest until the worm is off — so the worm is an ongoing condition as much as a fight,
  and either Rasp can attach it to a second target.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Palimpsest Wyrm">
  **Running it.** Written Over works before initiative: an attached wyrm knows everything its host
  knows and can speak in its voice. Erase suppresses limited-use features across a 30-foot Emanation
  for a round. It is the only inquiline a Lesser Restoration spell does not detach; removal takes
  the DC 19 Extraction check or a Greater Restoration spell.
</Creature>

### What is left

Two of the three diseases end with the host still walking. Neither creature below is a
parasite, and neither has one in it any more:

<Creature name="Lacuna">
  **Running it.** A lacuna's stat block exists for one moment: Drawn to Casting sends it, without
  malice, at whoever last cast a spell, past everyone else in the room. Empty Casting deals no
  damage because nothing happens when a lacuna casts. It cannot be infected and cannot be read, and
  the Lazaret uses lacunas to carry things through country nobody else will enter. They do not
  object to anything.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Amanuensis">
  **Running it.** The amanuensis is built as a social encounter with a stat block underneath it.
  Insists means divination confirms its story, because from the inside the story is true — a Zone of
  Truth spell clears it. Borrowed Hands is intended as one unexpected competence per scene, and its
  weapon is the clearest surviving trace of the person it believes it is.
</Creature>

### One who hunts them

The lancebill is not an inquiline. It is in this library because a party dealing with
one will meet it, and because it can remove a graft:

<Creature name="Lancebill">
  **Running it.** Lance is a cure delivered by something that does not stop at one: it takes a graft
  out of an unconscious ally, a prisoner, or the party's healer with the same stroke, and keeps
  hunting afterward because there is usually more than one. Parasite Sense means a grafted party in
  wet country is already being tracked, and Standing means the tracker is not seen until it moves.
</Creature>

### The apex

<Creature name="Exemplar">
  **Running it.** Seed grafts the whole party within three rounds regardless of rolls, so the fight
  is not the expensive part of this encounter — what the party carries out of it is. Take In and
  Draw remove up to four characters from the fight while Never Stopped regenerates through
  everything except Fire and Radiant damage. The First means every graft the party has ever carried
  descends from this creature, and that it perceives through all of them.
</Creature>
