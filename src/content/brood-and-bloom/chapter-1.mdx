All the creatures of the three broods are parasites. A parasite is a living organism.
In a host, it eats its way through for sustenance and growth.
Common folk may call the results of its presence "disease" out of ignorance;
these are not diseases and not curses, and they cannot be treated as either. In this
library, we use the word "disease" as well where it is the practical word — because it
is the word a victim uses.

The practical consequence is that healing a victim does nothing. A body carrying a
parasite is not wounded; it is inhabited. The organism has to come out — extracted,
starved, driven off, or burned off the land — and each brood's chapter says how.

## Growth & reproduction

The three broods reproduce in three different ways and on three different clocks. This
section collects all of it in one place; each brood's own chapter covers the rest.

### Inquiline

A latchling grows where it feeds: after 1d4 + 2 days attached, it becomes the form
its host's highest ability score sets, and every form after it grows the same way —
attached and feeding — until a sated adult leaves its host to lay:

| The host's highest ability score     | First form    | Later stages                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution | Mudspit Cyst  | Sallow Fluke, Mire Ambulant, Quagdam |
| Intelligence                         | Chantry Louse | Psalter Tick, Choirgall              |
| Wisdom or Charisma                   | Gaol Worm     | Palimpsest Wyrm                      |

Every sated adult lays a clutch of latchlings on its line's ground — still fresh water
for the sallow line, spell-soaked places for the chantry, wherever the dead are kept
for the palimpsest — and the cycle begins again.

Grafts are the second route. A graft is a cutting of its parent, and one that reaches
stage 4 of its disease emerges as a full member of its line, skipping the small forms:
a sallow graft matures in the corpse into a Quagdam, a chantry graft withdraws as a
Psalter Tick, and a palimpsest graft uncoils as a Palimpsest Wyrm. A graft whose host
dies earlier starves with it — a chantry or palimpsest graft dies within 1d4 days of
its host, while a sallow graft keeps eating and matures in the corpse into a Quagdam.

### Sporophore

A driftling that settles undisturbed for 24 hours becomes the stage 2 creature for
whatever it landed on:

| Substrate        | Line      | Stage 2        |
| ---------------- | --------- | -------------- |
| Wet tissue       | Rotgill   | Rotgill Fleece |
| Burned ground    | Ashcap    | Ashcap Crust   |
| Stone and timber | Reredos   | Reredos Rind   |
| A living person  | Orcshroom | Spore-Struck   |

From the mat, each line grows on its own ladder — the mats spread, fruiting bodies
rise out of established mats, and the apexes are not a stage any single creature
reaches but a thing an old colony becomes:

| Line      | Mat            | Fruiting body  | The colony becomes                                                                      |
| --------- | -------------- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rotgill   | Rotgill Fleece | Rotgill Bell   | A Miasm — condensed out of a colony that filled a flooded, sealed place                 |
| Ashcap    | Ashcap Crust   | Ashcap Censer  | A Burnt Acre — a colony burned over enough times that burning became part of it         |
| Reredos   | Reredos Rind   | Reredos Corbel | A Nave — a colony that took a whole structure and stopped being distinguishable from it |
| Orcshroom | —              | —              | A Rootfather — the tribe's eldest, taken root                                           |

The orcshroom line grows through people rather than places: whelps are born to the
tribe, Metaplasia recruits everyone else, and the Spore-Struck is the swifter taking of
a living body by a settled driftling. The intermediate fighting stages of every line —
stalkers, veils, blightboars, cinderwalks, the chorus, the buttress — are colonists of
particular bodies and places, described with their stat blocks.

### Necrophore

One loop, one method, one clock:

| Adult           | Lays in          | Larvae             | Time          |
| --------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ | ------------- |
| Emberwing       | Any fresh corpse | 1d4 Cinder Nits    | 1d6 + 2 hours |
| Tallow Imago    | Any fresh corpse | 1d4 Gravewax Grubs | 1d6 + 2 hours |
| Reliquary Imago | Any fresh corpse | 1d4 Crypt Instars  | 1d6 + 2 hours |

A larva reduced to 0 Hit Points by anything but its line's weakness seals into a husk
and opens as its adult 1 hour later — metamorphosis, not reproduction; no new creature
is added by it. Two lines carry intermediate growth: a Crypt Instar that feeds on the dead for 30 days
grows into a Sepulchre Nymph before any pupation, and gravewax grubs in a rich
larder fuse into a Gravewax Mass or settle into a Tallow Font, either of which spills
grubs again when broken. The elders — the Pyre Matron and the Catafalque Moth — are
adults that outlived enough layings, laying on the same schedule as every other adult.
Wakelight Wisps are shed by adults and grow into nothing. A Lych Lantern is a husk that
failed, and a Lychfield is what a killing ground becomes when the whole loop runs in
one place for generations.

## What magic does

Spells and features that restore Hit Points do nothing against any brood disease. Six
spells a party is likely to have already are useful, and what each does differs by brood:

| Spell                     | Inquiline                                                           | Sporophore                                                         | Necrophore                                           |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Detect Poison and Disease | Finds every parasite and graft in range, and names its line         | Reveals a creature's Spore Load, its line, and any disease's stage | Marks every seeded corpse in range                   |
| Lesser Restoration        | Detaches a lesser parasite, and works Depth loose early             | Removes 3 Spore Load, and recedes an early disease                 | Nothing to cure                                      |
| Greater Restoration       | Works Depth loose at any stage short of 4                           | Cures early, reduces late                                          | Nothing to cure                                      |
| Heal                      | As Greater Restoration, and works more Depth loose                  | As Greater Restoration, and reduces stage 3 to stage 1             | Nothing to cure                                      |
| Mass Heal                 | As Heal, for every creature it heals                                | As Heal, for every creature it heals                               | Nothing to cure                                      |
| Gentle Repose             | Pauses a dead host's clocks — the graft neither starves nor matures | Pauses a stage 4 corpse's clock                                    | Prevents seeding, and pauses a seeded corpse's clock |

The exact amounts are in each brood's chapter. Three points apply everywhere:

- **Detect Poison and Disease is the cheap answer.** Most of what these creatures do
  is invisible for the first several days; the spell finds it anyway. Against the
  Necrophore it marks corpses that have been laid in — the only warning the brood
  gives.
- **Protection from Poison slows nothing down.** It grants Advantage on saving throws
  against the Poisoned condition, which several stages impose, and makes a bad stage
  more survivable.
- **One caution.** Against the chantry line of the Inquiline, the four restoration
  spells in the table feed the disease instead of treating it. Only Detect Poison and Disease is safe on a chantry case. The rule is in
  [chapter 4](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-4/#chantry-drought).

### Spells written for these creatures

Three groups have written magic of their own about the broods, and none of the three
publishes: the Lazaret's counter-work, the Hands of the Host's workings, and whatever a
lone practitioner leaves in a notebook. [Chapter 7](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-7/#magic)
holds twenty-three of them. None is sold, taught, or learned at a level-up without a source
in front of the character, so a party has only what a Game Master has placed.

What they add to the table above:

| Brood      | What the workings reach                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| All three  | Anamnesis reads one body; Sequestration seals an area no brood creature can cross; Lazaret Sill closes a doorway to the small ones                               |
| Inquiline  | Extirpation ends a case outright; Prosector's Purgation buys Depth back at a price; Assumption of the Case moves it onto a willing caster; Countenance hides one |
| Sporophore | Bloom Interdict suppresses contaminated ground, and Glebe makes more of it                                                                                       |
| Necrophore | Unction keeps a corpse from being laid in, Discharge ends one, and Brood Inquest counts what a field is already carrying                                         |

Two of them change what the rest of this chapter says. Extirpation is the only thing in
this library that ends an Inquiline case at any stage short of 4 without a procedure, and
what it leaves standing in the room is the reason the order has never published it.
Countenance is why a case can walk through a house unexamined: it suppresses every penalty
of a stage while the graft goes on deepening underneath, and no Wisdom (Medicine) check
finds it.

The caution above extends to all of them. A chantry case gains Depth from any spell slot it
expends, so a grafted caster working through this list is feeding the graft with every
casting, whichever spell it was.

## Stage 4

Every brood disease ends at stage 4, and no medicine, procedure, or restoration spell
in this library reaches a stage 4 case. The reason is the one that makes restoration
magic work at all: it throws out what is still foreign. A parasite gripping a host, a
graft rooted in one, a fungal growth settled in a body — each is tissue a body can be
made to reject, which is why an unfinished case can be cured outright, and why the
growth in a spore-struck victim comes out with a single Lesser Restoration spell.
Stage 4 is where that stops being true: the change has completed, and what stands
there is not a victim with something in it but the creature itself. Two rules govern
what is left:

- **The window.** Every stage 4 leaves 1d4 days between the end of the disease and the
  arrival of the creature it produces, and a Gentle Repose spell cast on the body
  pauses the window for the spell's duration. Where stage 4 kills, the victim is an ordinary
  corpse during that window and a Resurrection spell works normally. Where stage 4
  leaves the victim alive, Resurrection has nothing to work on — reaching the same
  outcome means killing them first and raising them inside the same window.
- **After the window.** Once the creature has formed, what can be destroyed is a brood
  creature, and raising it raises a brood creature. What remains is a Wish spell, the
  direct intervention of a god, or a pact with something powerful enough to overrule
  the outcome.

Nothing in this library reaches into stage 4. One spell walks into it: a caster at stage 3
who casts [Preferment](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-7/#s-preferment) arrives there deliberately,
and everything above follows on schedule.
