Everything in this library was recorded by the Lazaret, an order of physicians and
archivists that has been cataloguing the broods for longer than anyone can remember.
This chapter covers the order itself, the classifications the rest of the library is
written in, what magic does against the broods, and the rule for the cases nothing
cures. The terms defined here — the broods, the lines, and the two counters — are used
by the stat blocks, so this chapter comes first.

## What these creatures are

A parasite is a living organism. In a host, it eats its way through for sustenance and
growth. Common folk may call the results of its presence "disease" out of ignorance;
these are not diseases and not curses, and they cannot be treated as either. In this
library, we use the word "disease" as well where it is the practical word — because it
is the word a victim uses.

The practical consequence is that healing a victim does nothing. A body carrying a
parasite is not wounded; it is inhabited. The organism has to come out — extracted,
starved, driven off, or burned off the land — and each brood's chapter says how.

## The order

A lazaret is a house where the sick are kept apart from everyone else. The order took
its name from one and never stopped being one. It began as a single quarantine house
during an outbreak that no longer has a name; the house came through it by a method its
physicians could not repeat, and what they wrote down afterward became the first file.
That house still stands and is still in use. The order calls it the first house, and
everything the Lazaret does now grew out of the habit of writing things down.

The Lazaret does not believe the broods can be destroyed. It believes they can be
described, and that a thing described accurately enough can be avoided, treated, or
survived. Its houses take in the infected, its officers travel to find more, and its
archive keeps what is learned from both. No part of the order is organized around
killing anything.

### Houses

A lazaret house is a hospital, a school, and a records office in one building, usually
at the edge of a settlement rather than inside it. There are somewhere between forty
and sixty; nobody is certain, because houses are founded by officers in the field and
reported afterward, and some are never reported at all. A house treats anyone who
reaches it and does not turn out a patient it cannot cure.

### Ranks

The order has three ranks, and in a small house one person often holds two of them:

- **Prosector.** The field rank. Prosectors travel alone, arrive uninvited, and perform
  the surgeries and amputations described in this library. Most of its practical
  knowledge was first written down by a prosector.
- **Lector.** Runs a house. Lectors diagnose, teach, and decide who is admitted. A
  lector who never served as a prosector is rare and not much respected.
- **Registrar.** Keeps the files. Every house copies its records to the first house
  twice a year, and the first house copies nothing back unless it is asked.

No lector outranks another, the one in the first house included. The order has a center
because its records need one, not because anyone rules from it.

### Classifications

Inquiline, Sporophore, and Necrophore are Lazaret classifications, and so are most of
the creature names in this library — chantry, reliquary, palimpsest, psalter, censer,
corbel, reredos. The vocabulary is clerical and archival because the first registrars
were, and the order does not change a name once it has been filed. What everyone else
calls these things is shorter, and rarely the same word twice from one valley to the
next.

Two collective terms from the files are used by stat blocks in this library:

- A **brood creature** is any Inquiline, Sporophore, or Necrophore creature.
- A **brood disease** is any disease described in this library.

### The doctrine

For each brood, the Lazaret's field instruction is one line long. Each brood's chapter
explains its instruction in full:

| Brood      | The standing instruction                       |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Inquiline  | The parasite first, then the graft.            |
| Sporophore | Go at noon, and bring tools for letting it in. |
| Necrophore | Make no light, make no dead.                   |

## What magic does

Spells and features that restore Hit Points do nothing against any brood disease. Five
spells are useful, and what each does differs by brood:

| Spell                         | Inquiline                                                   | Sporophore                                                         | Necrophore                         |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| _detect poison and disease_   | Finds every parasite and graft in range, and names its line | Reveals a creature's Spore Load, its line, and any disease's stage | Marks every seeded corpse in range |
| _lesser restoration_          | Detaches a lesser parasite, and works Depth loose early     | Removes 3 Spore Load, and recedes an early disease                 | Nothing to cure                    |
| _greater restoration_, _heal_ | Works Depth loose at any stage short of 4                   | Cures early, reduces late                                          | Nothing to cure                    |
| _mass heal_                   | As _heal_, for every creature it heals                      | As _heal_, for every creature it heals                             | Nothing to cure                    |

The exact amounts are in each brood's chapter. Three points apply everywhere:

- **_Detect poison and disease_ is the cheap answer.** Most of what these creatures do
  is invisible for the first several days; the spell finds it anyway. Against the
  Necrophore it marks corpses that have been laid in — the only warning the brood
  gives.
- **_Protection from poison_ slows nothing down.** It grants Advantage on saving throws
  against the Poisoned condition, which several stages impose, and makes a bad stage
  more survivable.
- **One caution.** Against the chantry line of the Inquiline, the four restoration
  spells in the table feed the disease instead of treating it. Only _detect poison and
  disease_ is safe on a chantry case. The rule is in
  [chapter 2](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-2/#chantry-drought).

## Stage 4

Every brood disease ends at stage 4, and no medicine, procedure, or restoration spell
in this library reaches a stage 4 case. Two rules govern what is left:

- **The window.** Every stage 4 leaves 1d4 days between the end of the disease and the
  arrival of the creature it produces. Where stage 4 kills, the victim is an ordinary
  corpse during that window and a _resurrection_ spell works normally. Where stage 4
  leaves the victim alive, _resurrection_ has nothing to work on — reaching the same
  outcome means killing them first and raising them inside the same window.
- **After the window.** Once the creature has formed, what can be destroyed is a brood
  creature, and raising it raises a brood creature. What remains is a _wish_ spell, the
  direct intervention of a god, or a pact with something powerful enough to overrule
  the outcome.

## The officers

The three ranks share one piece of training, printed on each stat block as **The
Trained Eye**: a Lazaret officer reads grafts, Spore Load, lines, and disease stages on
sight. What differs is what each rank does with it.

<Creature name="Lazaret Registrar">
  **Running it.** The registrar's value is knowledge. The Files makes it a reference the party can
  question about any brood creature, and Recite and Amend support allies' saving throws against
  brood effects. It is not built to fight; the Lancet is for cases that stand up mid-examination.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Lazaret Lector">
  **Running it.** The lector runs treatment. Intervene removes the small quantities — 1 Depth, 1
  Spore Load, a Poisoned condition — and Direct hands an ally an immediate, Advantaged Extraction or
  Medicine check, so treatment can happen twice in a round. A lector rarely travels; meeting one
  usually means standing in its house.
</Creature>

<Creature name="Lazaret Prosector">
  **Running it.** A prosector is statted as dangerous because the Lazaret sends them where nobody
  else goes, and they come back. Extract removes an attached parasite with no check and no damage;
  Excise compresses a full surgery into one action, once a day. A prosector who has spent Excise can
  still perform every procedure in this library — its Medicine bonus meets each procedure's
  Difficulty Class without Advantage.
</Creature>
