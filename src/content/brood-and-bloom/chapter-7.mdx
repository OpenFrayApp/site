Two answers to the same problem, and neither is common. This chapter holds the preparations
an officer of the Lazaret carries in a case, and the spells three separate groups have
written about the broods and each kept to themselves.

Alchemy comes first because it needs nothing of the caster. Magic does very little about
what these creatures do to a body; alchemy does more, slowly, at a price, and out of
materials that have to be taken off the creatures themselves. That is why the order's field
work has always been a trade in jars rather than a laying on of hands.

## Alchemy

Everything under this heading can be made by a character with no spellcasting whatsoever.

### Materials

Every preparation is made from one of the three broods, and the differences between them
decide what a cell, a house, or a party can realistically keep on hand. A harvest is one
unit of working material. Harvesting one takes 1 minute with a blade and a container and
requires no ability check unless the source’s stat block says otherwise.

| Brood      | Working material                       | Stays workable          |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Sporophore | Dried spore, cap, crust, and fleece    | 30 days                 |
| Inquiline  | Tissue cut from a living parasite      | 1 hour from the harvest |
| Necrophore | Wax, tallow, and wakelight from a body | 24 hours from the death |

An Inquiline harvest is tissue cut from one living Inquiline. The material can be carried,
but remains workable only until 1 hour after it is cut. The creature must be willing,
Incapacitated, or Restrained throughout the harvest, and it can provide one harvest between
Long Rests.

A Sporophore harvest is one usable portion cut from one dead Sporophore whose body was not
damaged by Fire. Each body provides one harvest. A living Rotgill Fleece is the exception
to the death requirement and the usual harvesting procedure. Use its Sessile trait to cut
it from the surface beneath it. The severed portion provides one harvest whether the check
succeeds or fails. Sporophore material remains workable for 30 days.

A Necrophore harvest is wax, tallow, or wakelight taken from one dead Necrophore within 24
hours of its death. Each body provides one harvest, and the material ceases to be workable
when that 24-hour window ends.

Sporophore material is the only kind that stockpiles, which is why every house keeps a
cupboard of it and why the cheapest preparations are the ones most likely to be available.
Inquiline material can travel only as far as its 1-hour limit permits. Necrophore material
requires a fresh death, which is the whole cost of that line and the reason its preparations
are rare in the hands of anyone who is not already killing.

The brood named on a preparation is the material it is made from, not the brood it works
against. Several of the most useful entries here are made from one and used against
another, because what a material does to flesh matters more than which creature it came
off. Ashcap dries a body, so ashcap keeps a corpse from being worth laying in. Rotgill and
crust stink and stick, so they keep small things from taking hold. Neither fact has
anything to do with what the ashcap or the rotgill wanted.

### Making them

The brood named in a recipe is the harvest it consumes under Materials. Every harvest must
still be workable when crafting begins; once the work begins, it remains usable through the
listed crafting time. The character must use alchemist’s supplies or a herbalism kit, but
need not be proficient with the chosen tool. At the end of the listed time, make the
Intelligence check against the listed DC. The check adds the character’s Intelligence
modifier and, if the character is proficient with the chosen tool, their Proficiency Bonus.
A failed check spoils every harvest used in the attempt.

| Rarity   | Time    | DC  | Materials      | Yield         |
| -------- | ------- | --- | -------------- | ------------- |
| Common   | 1 hour  | 13  | One harvest    | 1d4 + 2 doses |
| Uncommon | 8 hours | 15  | One harvest    | 1d4 doses     |
| Rare     | 1 day   | 18  | Three harvests | One dose      |

### What they cost, and who makes them

The Lazaret does not make everything in this section. A house is a hospital, a school, and
a records office, and its cupboard holds what those three things need: diagnosis,
protection, palliation, and the removal of a graft without killing the patient. Officers
are trained on all of it, and a house sells at cost to anybody who asks, including people
it has good reason to suspect.

What a house will not make is anything whose only use is on a person. Four of the poisons
below fall there, and the order has never held a formula for any of them without arguing
about whether to burn it. They come from poisoners, from cells, and from the sort of trade
that operates two streets from a house and knows the officers by sight. Prices for those
run higher, because nothing undercuts them.

### Using a preparation

Use the existing rule for the equipment each preparation most resembles: a potion, a vial
of poison, a flask of oil, or an improvised weapon. An entry states an exception when it
needs one:

- **Drinking a draught** takes a Bonus Action, as drinking a potion does, and so does
  administering one to a creature within 5 feet.
- **Applying an unguent** to the user or to a creature within 5 feet takes a Utilize action.
  Anointing a corpse takes 1 minute.
- **Lighting a fume** takes a Utilize action. A fume must be set down or planted rather than
  carried, unless its entry says otherwise.
- **Coating a weapon** with a poison takes a Bonus Action and covers one weapon or up to
  three pieces of ammunition. The coating keeps its potency for 1 minute or until its damage
  is dealt, whichever comes first.
- **Throwing a poison or dust** replaces one attack of the Attack action. Treat a thrown
  preparation as an improvised weapon with a normal range of 20 feet and a long range of 60
  feet. Make a ranged weapon attack using Dexterity, adding the attacker’s Proficiency Bonus
  only if they are proficient with improvised weapons. On a hit, apply the preparation’s
  entry; on a miss, the dose is expended with no effect.

A preparation is not magical and is not affected by a Dispel Magic spell, an antimagic
field, or anything that detects magic. Nothing here can be identified by an Identify spell.
Working out what is in an unlabeled bottle takes a DC 15 Intelligence (Nature) check and,
for most of them, a willingness to taste it.

### Draughts

<Preparation name="Countenance">

No house stocks it and no house will make one. The order's position is that a preparation
which leaves a case looking well and feeling well, while the graft goes on exactly as it
would in somebody screaming, is worse than anything in the poisons below, because
everything in the poisons below announces itself. The formula is in part IV of the True
File and nowhere else that anybody has found. It changes hands for nothing, passed on by people who
are handed one themselves every morning.

Inquiline tissue boiled down and strained twice. The result is clear, faintly warm to the
hand however long it has stood, and never quite still: held to a light there is a slow
drift in it that does not settle. It has no smell and almost no taste going down, then
leaves something metallic in the back of the throat a minute later.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Vigil">

Inquiline tissue reduced over a low heat for most of a day and cut with rotgill bitters to
keep it from setting in the bottle. Nearly black, oily enough to coat the glass, and
violently bitter all the way down. Officers are taught to drink it in one go.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Ablution">

Crust and rotgill washed, reduced, and washed again. Cloudy white with a chalky sediment
that has to be shaken back in. Tastes of chalk and salt, and smells faintly of a cold
cellar.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Lavage">

Ashcap reduced to a colorless liquid that smells of hot stone and takes the skin off a lip.
Given within a minute of a feeding, before anything has rooted properly.

Prosectors carry two and offer one immediately, leaving the patient to decide whether to
drink it.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Theriac">

A full day of reduction from three harvested portions, ending in a thick brown liquid that
has to be warmed before it will pour. It tastes of very little and smells strongly of yeast,
and a house making one can be identified from the street.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Kenotic draught">

Living inquiline tissue, barely worked at all, suspended in clear fluid. Pale threads are
visible in it, and they move. It is faintly sweet and slightly warm.

</Preparation>

### Unguents

<Preparation name="Collyrium">

Dried ashcap ground into rendered fat. A pale gray paste, gritty, that smells of a cold
hearth and stings badly for the first minute. It leaves a gray smear under both eyes that
takes hours to come off, which is how a village knows an officer has been working.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="House pitch">

Crust and rotgill boiled down into a black tar. Tacky, slow to spread, and it does not wash
out of cloth. The smell is resinous and sour and carries twenty feet, and every hand in a
cell can name it with their eyes shut.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Chrism">

Ashcap in oil, thin enough to run. It stays warm in the jar and smells of dry ash and hot
stone. On skin it does nothing. On a body it stiffens the surface within the hour and
leaves it gray and light.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Sounding">

Necrophore wax thinned with lamp oil. Yellow, slightly luminous in a dark room, and it
smells of fat that has gone over.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Cerate">

Reredos, ground and blended into wax, which is the one useful thing that line has ever
provided. Hard, pale, and odorless until warmed, when it smells faintly of stone dust. It
sets across an open wound in seconds and holds.

</Preparation>

### Fumes

<Preparation name="Compline">

Rotgill fleece dried hard and wound onto a stick. Burns badly, smokes heavily, and smells
of wet rope. The cheapest thing in this section and the one most often in a pack.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Pastille">

Ashcap pressed into a hard cake with a little fat. Burns white and fast, and leaves a
bitter film on the teeth that lasts most of a day.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Wakelight taper">

Necrophore tallow poured around a wick of the same. Burns pale green, gives no heat, and
does not gutter in wind. The smell is sweet and thin and gets into clothing.

</Preparation>

### Poisons

<Preparation name="Bloom oil">

Orcshroom spore suspended in oil that separates within the hour and has to be rolled
between the palms before use. Yellow-brown, smells of bread going wrong, and dries on the
blade to a fine dust. The dust is what does the work.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Ankylotic salt">

Reredos crushed to a coarse gray grit. Odorless, tasteless, and slow, which suits a poisoner who
intends to be elsewhere when it takes effect.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Wakelight dust">

Wakelight ground fine while the body is still warm. Faintly luminous, weightless, and
impossible to brush off cloth.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Adipocere">

Rendered from a body's own wax over a low heat, then set. Pale, firm, and odorless in the
tin, though it warms to a paste in the hand and then smells sweet.

</Preparation>

<Preparation name="Sallow tincture">

Tissue taken from a sallow-line parasite and thinned until it is indistinguishable from
water. No color, no smell, no taste. It cannot be identified in a cup by any means short of
a Detect Poison and Disease spell.

</Preparation>

## Magic

Three groups have written spells for these creatures, and none of the three publishes.

The Lazaret teaches some spells to its officers. The remaining spells are held in the
counter-work at the first house, whose catalog has never left the building. The order argues
about this restriction in writing because it publishes everything else.

The True File reaches a cell only as a copy in the founder’s hand, and most cells have never
seen one. Its spells appear in part V.

A practitioner’s spell survives in a private notebook or spellbook, often wherever its
writer last worked. These practitioners work alone, with no shared order or doctrine.

A Game Master places these sources. None is available at a shop, in a temple, or from a
tutor. Finding a source makes its spell available under a class’s normal spell rules; it
does not teach or prepare the spell by itself. A Cleric, Druid, or Paladin can prepare a
listed spell after finding its source; cantrips still follow the class’s cantrip rules. A
Wizard can copy a listed spell from the source into a spellbook, then prepare it normally.
A Sorcerer or Warlock can choose a listed spell when a class feature lets them learn or
replace a spell.

### What casting costs

Most of these spells cost a slot and nothing else.

[Instar](#s-instar), [Preferment](#s-preferment), and
[Second Assignment](#s-second-assignment) require the caster to carry a graft before
casting. A caster without one can know or prepare these spells but cannot cast them.

[Assumption of the Case](#s-assumption-of-the-case) transfers Depth to the caster and gives
them a graft if they do not have one; Second Assignment transfers an existing graft between
creatures. Instar adds 1 Depth after using an existing graft, while Preferment advances an
existing case by one full stage.

[Chantry Tithe](#s-chantry-tithe) can add 1 Depth to a graft the caster already carries. The
payment is optional, and the spell works without it.

The [disease rules](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-4/#depth) apply to every graft and point of
Depth these spells add or transfer. Nothing here suspends the Depth gained from a Long Rest.
Preferment is the only listed spell that reaches stage 4 directly.

### Reading a spell’s origin

Each description is followed by a note naming where the spell comes from. The three labels
describe what a party can find and who can teach the spell:

- **The order’s.** Every officer leaves the school with some of it, and each of the three
  ranks is trusted with more. The [officers’ stat blocks](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-2/#officers)
  name what each rank prepares. A house can grant access to its own copies; the rest remain
  in the first house’s counter-work.
- **The True File.** A cell’s copy holds part V in the founder’s hand. A source might be a
  complete copy, a page removed from one, or a transcription kept by a former hand.
- **A practitioner’s.** A private notebook or spellbook, usually kept in the place where its
  writer worked. An abandoned workroom, an inherited spellbook, or a practitioner’s archive
  can hold one.

### What already casts them

Three creatures from the earlier chapters draw on this section. None of them changes
Challenge Rating, and none of them gains a spellcasting block it did not have:

- **[Orcshroom Sporespeaker](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-5/#c-orcshroom-sporespeaker)** casts
  Anamnesis at will and Glebe twice a day. Nothing it knows comes from the True File — a
  sporespeaker works alone, from a tradition that has never been written down, and Glebe is
  the one that puts ground under its control.
- **[Psalter Tick](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-4/#c-psalter-tick)** can spend a stolen slot on
  Exacerbation or Chantry Tithe. The tick feeds on spellcasting, and Chantry Tithe rewards a
  caster for standing near other casters who are casting.
- **[Choirgall](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-4/#c-choirgall)** casts Anamnesis at will and Bloom
  Interdict once a day. A choirgall casting Bloom Interdict looks like help; it is protecting
  the casters it feeds on from the ground, so that they keep casting.

The three Lazaret officers in [chapter 2](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-2/#officers) carry the
order's own share of this section, split by rank: the registrar surveys, the prosector works
in the field, and the lector holds what a house does behind its own doors.
