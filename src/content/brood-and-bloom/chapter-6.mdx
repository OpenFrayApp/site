The Lazaret classes the pupating brood as Necrophore; common folk call it wakelight,
for the light its adults carry. It is the only brood that wants nothing from the
living, and the one a party leaves behind, in its dead. The Lazaret's standing
instruction for it: **make no light, make no dead.**

## How a necrophore works

The brood is three lines of moths on one closed loop: adults lay in fresh corpses,
larvae climb out and eat the dead, and a larva killed by the wrong damage seals into a
husk and opens as an adult. Nothing is implanted in the living, nothing infects, and
nobody catches anything. What the brood needs is bodies, and a party moving through
its territory produces them faster than anything else in the world.

<Note>
  Nothing a line leaves behind hampers that line. Gravewax is Difficult Terrain for everything
  except the tallow line that laid it down, and a lychfield — every line's leavings at once — is
  Difficult Terrain for everything except Necrophores.
</Note>

### Reproduction

A corpse becomes seeded when an adult lays in it, and a seeded corpse takes no further
laying. An adult never gets inside a body — a moth the size of a fishing boat could not —
so it settles over the corpse and lays through its ovipositor. An eligible corpse has been
dead no longer than 24 hours and contains no
living sallow graft. A corpse with a living sallow graft cannot be seeded. Once its Quagdam
emerges, the remains are no longer eligible.

The chronology after a death is:

1. An adult that reaches an eligible, unseeded corpse lays immediately. An adult present at
   the death can reach it with its Seed the Dead Reaction.
2. Inside established colony ground, an eligible corpse that remains unseeded becomes
   seeded exactly 3 hours after death. This 3-hour rule applies only to established ground
   and replaces the adult-arrival roll there. It represents an adult laying while
   unobserved.
3. Outside established ground, 1d4 adults arrive 1d6 + 2 hours after a death that draws
   them: a minimum of 3 hours and a maximum of 8. If the corpse remains eligible and
   unseeded, the first adult to reach it lays; the others remain nearby and fight
   interference.
4. The larvae emerge 1d6 + 2 hours after laying: a minimum of 3 hours and a maximum of 8.

A corpse seeded by the fixed 3-hour rule therefore hatches 6–11 hours after death; a corpse
first reached by arriving adults hatches 6–16 hours after death. An adult present at the
death produces larvae 3–8 hours after death.

| Adult           | Lays in            | Larvae             |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| Emberwing       | An eligible corpse | 1d4 Cinder Nits    |
| Tallow Imago    | An eligible corpse | 1d4 Gravewax Grubs |
| Reliquary Imago | An eligible corpse | 1d4 Crypt Instars  |

The cinder nit and gravewax grub can pupate after hatching. A Crypt Instar must first feed
on the dead for 30 days and grow into a Sepulchre Nymph. [Pupation](#pupation-and-the-husk)
begins when one of these three final larval forms drops to 0 Hit Points from anything but
its line’s destructive damage.

<Note>
  **Adults lay outside a fight as well.** Seed the Dead resolves a laying the party can see. The
  established-ground rule resolves adult visits that happen out of sight.
</Note>

### Pupation and the husk

Three creatures in this chapter pupate: the cinder nit, the gravewax grub, and the
sepulchre nymph. Each is the last larval stage of its line, and each carries the
Pupation trait on its stat block. The trait always works the same way: reduced to 0 Hit
Points by anything except the damage its line cannot survive, the larva seals where it
stands as a husk, and 1 hour later the husk splits and the adult takes its first turn.

Each husk has a stat block of its own, at the end of this chapter:

| Line      | Husk                 | Destroyed by    | Opens as        |
| --------- | -------------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Ember     | Cinder Nit Husk      | Cold            | Emberwing       |
| Tallow    | Gravewax Grub Husk   | Cold or Thunder | Tallow Imago    |
| Reliquary | Sepulchre Nymph Husk | Radiant         | Reliquary Imago |

Nothing else husks: instars and masses are destroyed at 0 Hit Points like anything
living, and an adult, once opened, never seals again. During its hour a husk is immune
to every condition, takes no actions, and keeps its Vulnerability to its line's damage
the whole time.

<Note>
  **Failed husks.** Not every husk opens. In a rare few the change halts partway, and the light that
  should have flown stays where it is and roots. What that becomes is the [Lych
  Lantern](#c-lych-lantern).
</Note>

## A colony's territory

A Necrophore colony has no den and no center. Its territory is wherever corpses are. A
site becomes established ground when a Necrophore adult opens from a husk there. Established
ground uses the fixed seeding step under Reproduction and has no adult-arrival roll.

Adults navigate by warmth and by one another's light. Fresh death outside established
ground can draw them from nearby territory, and a Wakelight Wisp can reveal a corpse to
every adult within 1 mile.

A corpse burned, frozen, sunk in running water, or sealed from the air before seeding
produces nothing. Burning or freezing a seeded corpse before its larvae emerge also ends
the laying. The Lazaret pays for clearing every corpse from a battlefield before these
windows close.

### Reading the ground

The signs are leavings: split husks beside old bones, runnels
of gray wax down a stair, scorch-rings in grass nothing ever burned, small carcasses
wedged into thorn trees at head height — the last being not the brood but the thing
that hunts it. A creature that succeeds on a DC 13 Intelligence (Nature) or Wisdom
(Survival) check reads the signs, and the age of the oldest husk dates the colony.

### Resting and light in a colony's ground

Fire after dark in a colony's ground is a beacon, and so is a lantern.
A party that keeps a cold, dark camp passes the night beneath the colony's notice unless something nearby dies.

## The diseases

The Necrophore has no disease and no counter: nothing in this chapter infects a living
creature, and nothing here is cured. Of the spells in
[What magic does](/brood-and-bloom/chapter-1/#what-magic-does), two have a use against
this brood:

- Detect Poison and Disease marks every seeded corpse in range.
- Gentle Repose prevents a corpse from being seeded. An already seeded
  corpse's clock stops for the spell's duration, and a body can be carried home through
  the brood's own territory.
