A simple is a preparation of one plant, one fungus or one animal part, as against a
compound of several. Everything in this chapter is one thing, prepared by one of the six
methods in [the second chapter](/strong-waters/chapter-2/), and doing one thing to a body
that is worth knowing about.

They are arranged by family, by what they do rather than by name, because a practitioner
who has read the waking reads the quieting faster.

Every entry here has an imitation, and the imitations are in
[the fifth chapter](/strong-waters/chapter-5/). A practitioner who cannot tell one from the
other will treat the wrong condition, and the two do not resemble each other once the
treating starts.

## The measure

Every entry names its **measure**: one dose, defined per simple, because a mouthful of one
thing is not a mouthful of another. A thumb's width of dried lichen and three drops of a
tincture are each one measure of their own thing.

Entries say what one measure does, what a second does on top of it, and where the line is
past which a saving throw decides the matter. **A simple does not do a graded version of
the same thing at every dose.** Several do nothing at all until the second measure, and
several change what they do entirely.

Every entry also names what it adds to **Craving**, and what its excess is where it has one.
The count itself — how it converts, the three degrees of addiction it converts into, and
how one ends — is set out in [the first chapter](/strong-waters/chapter-1/) and is the same
for everything in this book.

## Overdose

Past a certain number of measures in one sitting, a simple stops being what it was. Each
entry names its threshold and its consequence. Some are unpleasant. Four in this chapter
kill, and each of those says so in its own entry, in plain words.

An overdose is the only thing in this chapter that kills a creature outright. What years of
a substance do to a body is written in the entries that have it and counted nowhere. It
happens too slowly to count in days.

### Smallweed

A broad gray-green leaf from a plant that grows wherever ground has been disturbed — spoil
heaps, roadsides, the edge of a yard — and that wants no tending, no season and no skill to
raise. It is cut in late summer and hung until it is leather. It smells of hay, and once
lit of something sweeter and worse that gets into cloth and stays there. It tastes of
scorched grass. The first of either that a creature takes in its life makes it sick and
dizzy for an hour, which is the only warning the plant gives.

It is smoked in one of two forms, and they are the same leaf. Shredded fine and rolled into
papers about the thickness of a straw, twisted shut at one end and lit at the other, it is
smoked in a few minutes. Grown in a warm country, cured slowly whole rather than shredded,
and wound about itself to a finger's thickness and a hand's length, it is a **goldleaf**,
and is cut at one end and smoked over an hour. Unlit a goldleaf smells of dried fruit and
cellar wood. Lit, it smells of smallweed, which is the tell.

A measure is one roll or one goldleaf, and it takes hold on the first draw. For 1 hour, a
creature that is Addicted to smallweed does not suffer the Disadvantage of going without
it. For any other creature it does nothing at all.

Rolls are sold made up rather than loose, in tens and twenties, with nothing taken off for
the larger packet. Goldleaf is sold singly and in boxes of 3, 5, 10 and 20 at any grade,
and a box costs its count with nothing taken off either — ten Cedar House goldleaves are
20 gp in a box, which is what ten cost bought one at a time. A box is a quantity and not a
measure.

It is common enough to be sold under a maker's name, and the name sets the price and
nothing else. Every line below is the same plant doing the same thing.

Rolled, priced for a packet of ten:

Two of the eleven are priced by the name. Weald Gold is the Blue Hand's leaf in a printed
wrapper at twice the money, and Montarre is Almadera's, grown on the next estate and aged
the same three years, at four times it.

The same packet costs what the house it is bought in can charge for it, so a Blue Hand is
2 sp at a poor house and 4 sp in a comfortable one, and a Cedar House goldleaf is sold at a
squalid stall for whatever the seller thinks can be got.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I have opened a Weald Gold and a Blue Hand on the same counter and asked the buyer to tell me
  which was which, and he could not, and he bought the Weald Gold. I do not think he was a fool. He
  was buying the wrapper and he knew it, and the wrapper was what he had come in for.
</Voice>

Each day on which a roll was taken adds 2 to Craving. Each day on which a goldleaf was
taken adds 1. The leaf is the same and the count is the same, and what differs is how much
of it a day holds: a day of rolls is a dozen or twenty, and a day of goldleaf is one. A
creature that smoked twenty goldleaves in a day would arrive exactly where the roll smoker
arrives, and would have spent forty gold getting there.

A day on which a creature took both adds the higher of the two and not the sum. It is one
count and one body.

Its excess is the smell and the light. A creature at the third degree lights one where it
should not — indoors where it is forbidden, on a watch, in a wood at night, in the hour
before a door it means to pass unnoticed — and it does not decide to. While a creature is
smoking, and for 1 hour afterward, it has Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made
against anything that can smell. In darkness a lit roll is visible at 300 feet, and further
at the moment it is drawn on. A creature at the third degree carries the smell in its
clothes and hair whether it is smoking or not, and that does not wash out overnight.

A goldleaf cannot be put out and relit without ruining it, so a creature at the third
degree does not cut one short and can be found wherever it can finish.

A creature that shares a goldleaf of Whole leaf grade or better with another creature, and
spends the hour over it, has Advantage on Charisma checks made toward that creature until
it is finished. A coarse one carries no such advantage, sharing one being no courtesy
anybody notices. That is the only thing a goldleaf does that a roll does not.

What it costs over thirty years — the cough that arrives and does not leave, the wind gone
by forty, the years off the end — is not counted anywhere in this book.

**Overdose.** A creature that smokes considerably more than it is accustomed to has the
Poisoned condition for 1 hour. No quantity of it kills a creature in a day.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  It is the only thing on my shelves nobody asks me about. They ask what the lichen will do to a
  heart and whether the poppy can be left off, and they buy smallweed the way they buy bread. I have
  sold it to the same men for thirty years, and I have heard what happened to their breathing, and
  not one of them has ever connected the two things in front of me.
</Voice>

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I have sold a goldleaf to a man who would not have smallweed in his house, and I did not tell him
  what it was, and I have thought about that since. It is the same leaf and the same debt in better
  clothes. What his money bought him was the hour it takes to smoke one, which is not nothing, and
  is not what he thought he was buying.
</Voice>

### Vapour

A brass vessel the size of a pear, holding a spoonful of sweetened spirit, warmed over a
small lamp and breathed through a tube. It came out of an apothecary's bench, where the
same apparatus carries a steam to a chest that will not clear, and the trade found the
other use for it within a season. The vessel costs 5 gp and lasts for years; a charge costs
what it holds.

A **charged** vapour has smallweed steeped into the spirit, and one charge is one measure
of smallweed. It shares smallweed's count, because it is smallweed. A **plain** vapour is
flavored spirit and nothing else — mint, aniseed, whatever the maker sells — and it does
nothing at all, carries no Craving, and is the only thing in this chapter that a creature
can take every day of its life for no cost.

What a charged vapour changes is not the wanting but the excess. It gives off a thin sweet
steam that is gone in a minute rather than a smoke that stays in cloth, and it shows no
ember. A creature taking a charged vapour does not suffer smallweed's Disadvantage on
Dexterity (Stealth) checks, and there is no light to be seen at 300 feet. Everything else
about the entry is unchanged: the count rises at the same rate, the degrees arrive on
schedule, and the creature at the third degree is still at the third degree.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  It is my own bench's apparatus put to a use I did not intend, and I have been asked whether that
  troubles me. It does not. What it takes away is the smell and the ember, which are the two things
  that ever caused a man to be told he was smoking too much.
</Voice>

### Drover's cud

A tough gray leaf from the verges of any road cattle are walked down, chewed flat by
drovers on the long stages and spat out at the inn. Dull green going gray, thick as
leather. It smells of hay and faintly of pepper. It tastes of nothing much for a while and
then sharply bitter, which is when a drover knows to spit it.

A measure is one leaf chewed for a minute. For 2 hours the creature has Advantage on
saving throws made to stay awake. A second leaf in that time does nothing at all, which is
the whole reason drovers hold it in such affection and apothecaries in so little.

Two measures in a day add 1 to Craving. There is no overdose; a body simply stops
answering it.

### Kindleseed

A hard black seed from a roadside spurge, useless raw and quite different roasted, which is
a thing somebody discovered by accident and everybody has known since. Glossy black,
chestnut brown once roasted. It smells of burnt sugar. It tastes bitter and oily, and it
leaves the mouth dry for an hour.

A measure is a dozen seeds. For 6 hours the creature has Advantage on saving throws made
to stay awake and gains no Exhaustion level from going without sleep. A second measure adds
Disadvantage on Wisdom checks as the attention narrows to whatever is in front of it.

**Overdose.** A third measure within 6 hours: DC 13 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature takes 7 (2d6) Psychic damage and has the Frightened condition for 1 minute,
frightened of nothing it can name.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 1 to Craving.

### Emberfoot

A lichen the color of old brick, which takes the warm stone about a spring or a smithy's
outer wall and will not take anything colder. It grows flat and scaled, paler at the edge
where it is newest, and wetted it darkens almost to black and the scales lift. It smells
faintly of hot iron, and of bread once the water is on it. The taste is sour first, and
then a heat that is not a burning but sits at the back of the throat and stays there a
while after. Miners chew it dry. Smiths reduce it, being particular people.

<Voice title="Of its preparing" by="Hesper Aldwine">
  One will gather the lichen from the warm side of the stone only, and leave what grows
  below the water line, for that is a different thing and will gripe.

One will bruise a good handful in a mortar, dry, until the scales part; then one will set
it in a pint of soft water and boil it over a slow and even fire until the third part be
wasted, which is four hours or near it. One will not suffer it to gallop, for a hard boil
takes the heat out of it and leaves only the sourness.

It is commonly said that a cantrip will hold the fire. It will warm the pot an hour, and
the boil wants four; one who trusts it will cast it thrice and still come back to a cold
pot, for warming is not boiling and never was. Steady Fire will do the work.

Strained through linen and stoppered, it will keep a twelvemonth. Unstrained it will keep
a month and then it will turn, and one will know it turned by the smell of bread going
out of it.

</Voice>

A measure is one mouthful of the reduction, or a thumb's width of the dried lichen chewed.
It takes hold within a minute. For 4 hours after taking one measure, a creature has
Advantage on saving throws made to stay awake and gains no Exhaustion level from going
without sleep. A second measure inside those 4 hours holds the waking and adds Disadvantage
on Dexterity checks, as the hands go unsteady. A third brings the heart up: the creature
must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or have the Poisoned condition for 1
hour.

**Overdose.** A fourth measure inside the same 4 hours calls a DC 15 Constitution saving
throw. On a failed save the creature takes 3 (1d6) Necrotic damage, gains 1 Exhaustion
level, and its Speed is halved for 1 hour.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 1 to Craving. Its excess is that a creature at
the third degree takes a measure at the start of any task it expects to be long, and there
is always another task.

### Wakerush

A river sedge with a hollow three-sided stem, cut at the waterline and infused. Pale green
going straw as it dries. It smells of cut grass and pond water. It tastes green, cold and
slightly soapy, and it is the pleasantest thing in this family to take and the hardest to
stop taking.

A measure is a cup of the infusion. For 8 hours the creature has Advantage on saving throws
made to stay awake, gains no Exhaustion level from going without sleep, and needs no food
in that time. A second measure extends nothing and adds Disadvantage on Constitution saving
throws.

**Overdose.** A third measure within 8 hours: DC 15 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature gains 2 Exhaustion levels when the wakerush ends, whenever that is.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is sleep: a creature at
the third degree does not take a night off, because a night off is a night behind.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  Wakerush is the reason I began keeping the count at all. It does everything it promises and it
  asks for the whole of it back, and the people it has cost most were sober, careful, hard-working
  people who were simply behind on something.
</Voice>

### Quickthorn

The inner bark of a black thorn that grows in hedges nobody planted, tinctured in aqua
vitae for a month. Deep red-brown in the bottle. It smells of almonds and iron. It tastes
of almonds and then of blood, and it acts before the bottle is stoppered.

A measure is five drops on the tongue. It takes hold at once. For 1 hour the creature has
Advantage on Initiative rolls and on Dexterity saving throws, and its Speed increases by
10 feet. A second measure in that hour adds Disadvantage on Wisdom and Charisma checks; the
world arrives faster than judgment does.

**Overdose.** A third measure within 1 hour: DC 16 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature takes 14 (4d6) Necrotic damage as the heart is driven past what it will take,
and drops to 0 Hit Points if this damage reduces it below half its Hit Point maximum.

Each measure adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is money — a creature at the third degree spends
what it has on it, and then what it has not.

### Larkwort

A small yellow flower dried and smoked, taking hold in the time it takes to exhale. Pale
gold, brittle. It smells of honey when dry and of scorched honey when burning. It tastes
sweet and leaves the tongue faintly numb.

A measure is one pipe. For 30 minutes the creature has Advantage on Charisma checks and on
saving throws against being Frightened, and Disadvantage on Wisdom checks. A second pipe
in that time replaces the Advantage on Charisma checks with Disadvantage, as the talk runs
past where anybody wanted it.

**Overdose.** A third pipe within 30 minutes: DC 14 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature has the Poisoned condition for 1 hour and cannot stop talking, which has cost
more people more than the poison has.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 3 to Craving. Its excess is company: a creature
at the third degree will not sit down with anybody without a pipe lit, and cannot settle to
sleep without one.

### Nightsmith

A black fungus from the roof timbers of forges, gathered where the smoke has been going up
for thirty years or more, and decocted at length. Black, and it stains everything. It
smells of soot and yeast. It tastes of ash and salt.

A measure is a spoonful of the decoction, taken an hour before it is wanted. For 12 hours
the creature has Advantage on saving throws made to stay awake, gains no Exhaustion level
from going without sleep, and has Advantage on Intelligence checks. A second measure in
that time gives no further benefit and imposes the Poisoned condition for the remainder.

**Overdose.** A third measure within 12 hours: DC 17 Constitution saving throw. **On a
failure the creature dies**, and she says plainly that she has seen it and that it is not
quick. On a success it gains 3 Exhaustion levels and is Unconscious for 1d6 hours.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 3 to Craving. Its excess is work — a creature at
the third degree takes a measure before anything it cares about doing, and comes to believe
it cannot do the thing without one.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  Nightsmith is the finest thing in this family and I have sold perhaps two dozen doses in my life,
  all of them to people I knew by name and had refused at least once. It will carry a scholar
  through a fortnight. What it does after the fortnight is in the seventh chapter, and I have
  written that part at length.
</Voice>

## The quieting

Simples that put pain down, or sleep up. The family a practitioner uses most and worries
about most, and the one that fills the seventh chapter.

| Simple        | Form          | Onset      | Duration  | Craving | Price  |
| ------------- | ------------- | ---------- | --------- | ------- | ------ |
| Bell-bind     | Infusion      | 30 minutes | 8 hours   | 1       | 3 sp   |
| Marrowmoss    | Unguent       | 10 minutes | 4 hours   | 1       | 1 gp   |
| Hushcap       | Dried, chewed | 20 minutes | 6 hours   | 2       | 6 gp   |
| Dulcet root   | Decoction     | 30 minutes | 8 hours   | 2       | 15 gp  |
| Sleepwrack    | Infusion      | 10 minutes | 1d4 hours | 2       | 25 gp  |
| Milk poppy    | Tincture      | 10 minutes | 4 hours   | 4       | 50 gp  |
| Sister's ease | Tincture      | Immediate  | 1 hour    | 5       | 120 gp |

### Bell-bind

A hedge convolvulus with a white trumpet flower, the root dug in autumn and infused. Pale,
starchy, unpromising. It smells of raw potato. It tastes of almost nothing, which is why
it goes into so many other things.

A measure is a cup at bedtime. The creature falls asleep within 30 minutes and sleeps 8
hours, and its Long Rest is not interrupted by ordinary noise or discomfort. It is woken
normally by anything that would wake a sleeping creature in danger.

There is no overdose worth the name; a second cup simply tastes worse. Three nights running
add 1 to Craving. Its excess is that a creature at the third degree cannot fall asleep
without a cup, and will lie awake the whole night rather than try.

### Marrowmoss

A gray moss from the north side of standing stones, worked into rendered fat and rubbed
into a joint. Grey-green, greasy once made up. It smells of earth and tallow. It is not
taken by mouth and tastes, she notes, exactly as bad as it looks.

A measure is a thumb of the unguent rubbed in. For 4 hours the treated limb aches less. A
creature with an injury that would impose Disadvantage on checks using that limb ignores
that Disadvantage for the duration. It heals nothing and knits nothing.

**Overdose.** None by this route. A creature that eats it — and she records two — has the
Poisoned condition for 8 hours and learns not to.

Three days' use adds 1 to Craving.

### Hushcap

A fawn mushroom with a skirt, found in beech mast, dried hard and chewed slowly. Fawn going
brown, leathery. It smells of dust and old paper. It tastes of nothing and then, some
minutes later, of sweetness.

A measure is one cap. For 6 hours the creature has Resistance to the pain of injury: it
does not gain Disadvantage from wounds and cannot be forced to make a check or save to
withstand pain. It also has Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks, being some distance
from its own skin.

**Overdose.** A second cap within 6 hours: DC 14 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature has the Unconscious condition for 1d4 hours and does not wake for damage.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is that a creature at
the third degree takes a cap at the first ache of anything, including aches it would not
have noticed before it started.

### Dulcet root

A pale forked root from wet ground, decocted for six hours until the liquor goes syrupy and
faintly gold. It smells of aniseed and cellars. It tastes intensely sweet and then bitter,
and the bitterness is how one knows it is finished.

A measure is a spoonful. For 8 hours the creature ignores the effects of one Exhaustion
level, and has Advantage on saving throws against being Frightened or Charmed. A second
measure adds the Poisoned condition for the duration.

**Overdose.** A third measure within 8 hours: DC 15 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature has the Paralyzed condition for 1d4 hours, awake throughout, which she notes is
the detail people are not told and should be.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is fear: a creature at
the third degree takes a spoonful before anything it expects to be frightened by, which
comes in time to be most things.

### Sleepwrack

A brown weed from tidal rock, gathered at the spring low and infused. Olive-brown, rubbery,
smelling strongly of the sea. It tastes of salt and iodine and is thoroughly unpleasant.

A measure is a cup. The creature must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution saving throw or fall
Unconscious for 1d4 hours. On a success it is not affected at all. Damage does not wake it;
an hour of vigorous effort by somebody else will.

This is the surgeon's draught, and the entry says so: it is taken to be cut, not to rest.

**Overdose.** A second cup while the first holds: DC 17 Constitution saving throw. **On a
failure the creature stops breathing and dies** within a minute unless somebody with the
means and the wit intervenes.

Each use adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is the worst in the chapter, because a second cup
kills: a creature at the third degree ends any day it did not like with one, and does not
always remember whether it has had one already.

### Milk poppy

The seed head of a tall gray poppy, scored at dusk and the milk gathered at dawn, dried and
then tinctured. The gum is white going brown; the tincture is dark and bitter. It smells
faintly of hay and strongly of itself, which is a smell nobody who knows it mistakes. It
tastes appallingly bitter through any amount of honey.

<Voice title="Of its preparing" by="Hesper Aldwine">
  One will score the head shallow and across, and one will come back at first light, and
  what has wept out overnight is what one has. It is a day's work for a thimble and it
  cannot be hurried by any means I know of, and I include the eighth chapter in that.

One will tincture it in clean aqua vitae, never in wine, and one will label the bottle
with the date and with what it is, in letters somebody frightened can read. I have known
three houses where the unlabeled bottle was the whole of the tragedy.

</Voice>

A measure is three drops. For 4 hours the creature ignores the effects of two Exhaustion
levels, cannot be forced to make a check or save to withstand pain, and has Advantage on
saving throws against being Frightened. It also has Disadvantage on all Wisdom checks and
its Speed is reduced by 10 feet.

A second measure within 4 hours gives no further relief and adds the Poisoned condition.

**Overdose.** A third measure within 4 hours: DC 16 Constitution saving throw. **On a
failure the creature's breathing slows and stops.** It falls Unconscious and begins making
death saving throws at the start of its next turn, with no wound to find and nothing to
stanch. On a success it is Unconscious for 1d6 hours.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 4 to Craving. Its excess is that a creature at
the third degree takes a measure at the first sign of pain of any kind, including pain it
is only expecting.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I have never once regretted giving milk poppy to somebody dying and I have regretted nearly every
  other time. It is the best thing in this book and it takes the most, and a practitioner who is
  casual with it will find out which of those two facts arrives first.
</Voice>

### Sister's ease

A tincture of milk poppy reduced and redistilled until a measure is three drops of
something several times its strength — an apothecary's improvement on an apothecary's
drug. Nearly black, syrupy. It smells and tastes of milk poppy and worse.

A measure is one drop. It acts at once and does everything milk poppy does, for 1 hour,
without the Disadvantage on Wisdom checks — which is precisely the problem, because
nothing about it announces itself.

**Overdose.** A second drop within the hour: DC 18 Constitution saving throw, with the same
failure as milk poppy and no warning beforehand. **This kills more practitioners than
patients**, and the entry says so.

Each use adds 5 to Craving. Its excess is milk poppy's, arriving sooner and with nothing to
announce it.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I made this. I am not going to pretend otherwise in my own book. It was for a woman with
  a crushed leg and four days to live and it was the right thing on that occasion, and it
  has since been copied by people who had neither her circumstances nor my arithmetic.

If a reader takes one thing from this catalog let it be that the strong version of a good
thing is not a better good thing. It is a different thing, and it wants a different sort
of care, and I did not understand that at forty as well as I understand it now.

</Voice>

## The seeing

Simples that change what a creature sees, or show it something else in place of the room.
Nothing here holds sleep off or puts pain down. They are taken to look, and the family
does not resemble the rest of the catalog in what it asks: its counts are small, and
three of its seven entries carry none at all. A body does not come to expect these the
way it learns to expect the rest, and she records plainly that she cannot say why.

Nothing in this family kills by its own hand, and most of what goes wrong under it is
answered by the first rung of the seventh chapter's ladder: water, warmth, and somebody
sober in the room until it ends.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  People come to my counter for this family with a different face. Nobody has ever asked me for
  milk poppy with their chin up, and the veilcap buyers look me in the eye and tell me at length,
  and what they are buying, as near as I can make out, is for something to mean something. I sell
  it. In forty years I have not settled whether it works, and I notice that nobody has ever come
  back and told me it failed.
</Voice>

| Thornapple | Infusion      | 1 hour     | 8 hours    | —       | —     |

### Veilcap

A fawn mushroom on a slender stem, up in dunged pasture a few mornings after autumn rain
and gone by noon of the third day, dried hard and chewed. Fawn going gray, and it bruises
blue where the fingers handle it. It smells of meal. It tastes of meal and of mud, and
nobody has ever claimed to take it for the taste.

A measure is one cap. For 4 hours the world patterns itself: colors carry weight, sounds
carry shapes, and whatever the creature looks at looks back with intent. It has
Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks for the duration, and it automatically fails
any check made to see an illusion for what it is. A second cap in those hours deepens
nothing and lengthens nothing; a body answers veilcap or it does not.

**Overdose.** A third cap within 4 hours: DC 13 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature takes 7 (2d6) Psychic damage and has the Frightened condition for 1 hour,
frightened of one particular thing it saw, which it can name and cannot stop seeing.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 1 to Craving. Most of its buyers take it twice
a year, at a fair or a festival, and their count never moves at all.

### Mothlight

A papery gray-white lichen off old orchard bark, in scales like a moth's wing folded
flat, dried and smoked in a short pipe. It smells of dust in the jar and of incense
burning, and the smoke is thick, sweet, and pale enough to hold light — a pipe of it in a
dark room seems to give more than the embers account for.

A measure is one pipe. For 30 minutes every light the creature can see wears a halo, the
dark between lights is a kindly dark, and nothing in either is worth being afraid of. It
has Advantage on saving throws against being Frightened, and Disadvantage on attack
rolls, having lost interest in the outcome. A second pipe within the half hour extends
the whole of it to 1 hour and adds Disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws.

**Overdose.** A third pipe within 1 hour: DC 13 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the creature is Charmed by every creature it can see, for 1 hour, and it remembers
afterward exactly what that was like.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is the dark: a
creature at the third degree keeps to it, arranges to sleep by day, and stops lighting
lamps in its own rooms.

### Dreamvine

A twining hedge vine with milk-white flowers that open at dusk, the flowers gathered the
same evening and tinctured, since dried they are nothing. The tincture is faintly gold.
It smells of the flower, green and cold. It tastes of very little, and it is taken at
bedtime.

A measure is three drops. The creature sleeps its ordinary sleep and completes a Long
Rest as it otherwise would, and it dreams in order — whole, sequential, and kept: on
waking it remembers the night entire, in the sequence it happened. The dreams are its
own. Nothing in the vine sends anything, and she is plain on the point: a creature that
dreamed an answer had the answer.

**Overdose.** None found. More of it deepens nothing; a night holds the dreams it holds.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is the mornings: a
creature at the third degree wakes disappointed, goes to bed early to get back, and comes
to prefer the ordered nights to the disordered days.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  Half my dreamvine goes to diviners, and I sell it to them with the same sentence every time: the
  vine is a scribe and not a messenger. Some of them thank me. The ones who come back weekly have
  generally stopped hearing the sentence, and I have watched a sensible woman rearrange a year
  around what a Tuesday told her.
</Voice>

### Ditchking

The dried milk of a heavy brown toad that winters in field drains and is found nowhere
else, wiped from its back where it beads when the toad is handled, dried on glass and
smoked. A toad gives a scraping a season and the toads are few, which is most of the
price. The dried milk is amber, glassy, and nearly scentless. Smoked, it tastes of burnt
sugar and then of nothing, because the creature has left.

A measure is one scraping, and it acts on the first draw. For 10 minutes the creature is
entirely elsewhere: it has the Incapacitated condition, perceives nothing of its
surroundings, and cannot be roused by anything short of damage. It returns all at once,
unharmed, and the reports agree with one another to a degree she found remarkable — a
light, a largeness, and being glad.

A second measure the same day does nothing at all. A body answers ditchking once between
dawns, and no preparation she tried moves that.

**Overdose.** None. A larger measure buys the same 10 minutes.

It carries no Craving. She kept the count on eleven regular buyers across twenty years,
it never moved for any of them, and she offers no explanation.

### Loomsight

A gray root like a knot of cord, from a low plant of high field margins that flowers once
and dies in the digging. It is three years growing to a usable root, and the root does
not divide. Decocted for a morning, it gives a liquor the color of weak tea that smells
of wool and rain, and tastes woolen too, and faintly sweet.

<Voice title="Of its preparing" by="Hesper Aldwine">
  One will take the whole root or leave the whole root, for it does not divide: cut, the virtue is
  out of both halves by morning. And one will make peace with the arithmetic before one digs, for
  the plant is three years growing and dies in the lifting.

Hasten the Root will raise one from seed in an afternoon, and what one has raised is one plant,
which is one measure. The spell spares the three years. It does not change the count.

</Voice>

A measure is a cup. For 1 hour the creature attends, completely, to what is in front of
it. It has Advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks, and Charisma (Deception) checks made
against it are made with Disadvantage. For the same hour it is nowhere else: it has
Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks against anything other than what it is
attending to, and on Initiative rolls. A second cup within the hour adds nothing and
costs the stomach the rest of the day.

**Overdose.** A third cup within a day: DC 14 Constitution saving throw. On a failure the
creature takes 7 (2d6) Psychic damage and has the Incapacitated condition for 1 hour,
attending to everything equally, which is attending to nothing.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is the asking: a
creature at the third degree will not read a face, weigh an offer or judge a stranger
without a cup, and has stopped trusting what its own eyes do for free.

### Greencrown

A green flower in the shape of a small crown, on high heath, open for one week at
midsummer and worthless dried — the week is the price. It is infused fresh, a whole
flower to a cup. The infusion is green as bottle glass. It smells of cut stems, and it
tastes bitter, long, and organized, which is her word and the only one she offers.

A measure is the cup, and everything about its sale assumes what every seller of it
insists on: a minder, a quiet room, and an empty day to follow. For 8 hours the creature
is taken apart in front of itself. It has Disadvantage on all ability checks for the
duration, it cannot maintain Concentration on a spell, and it goes nowhere without being
led. At the end it must make a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw: on a success it keeps the whole
of the 8 hours, in order; on a failure it keeps fragments.

**Overdose.** A second cup, that week or that season, does nothing. The flower spends a
body entirely on the first.

It carries no Craving. Almost nobody takes greencrown twice in a year, and most never
take it twice at all.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I have sat as minder nine times and drunk it once. Mine was thirty years ago, I have the whole
  of it still, and I have never wanted the second cup — which I can say about nothing else on my
  shelves, and which I count the most honest fact in this catalog.
</Voice>

### Thornapple

The one entry in this family under its real name, because what it does is what the plant
does and no slot in the catalog asked for it. A rank waist-high weed of farmyards and
middens, with a white trumpet flower that opens at evening and a spined seed pod the size
of a hen's egg. Every part of it acts, the seeds hardest. It smells rank and green. It is
not sold so much as met, and the entry exists because meeting it goes badly.

A measure is hard to state, which is the first thing wrong with it: pods vary threefold
in strength, and the line between the effect and the overdose runs inside a single pod. A
creature that has taken it is, for 8 hours, somewhere adjacent to the world. It has
Disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls, it automatically fails any check made to
see an illusion for what it is, and it holds conversations with creatures that are not
present and accepts them as present. It does not know it has taken anything. That is the
mark of thornapple against the rest of the family: veilcap shows a creature more than is
there, and thornapple takes away the part that could tell.

**Overdose.** A second pod, or a strong first one: DC 15 Constitution saving throw. On a
failure the duration becomes 1d4 days, and the creature gains 1 Exhaustion level at the
end of each of them. It does not kill by its own hand. She records five deaths under it,
and every one was a drowning, a fall, or a night outdoors in winter — the window taken
for a door, the pond taken for a path.

Its Onset is an hour, the widest window in the chapter: a creature made to vomit inside
it takes none of the above. It carries no Craving, and she records no creature that took
it twice by choice.

## The steadying

Simples that still what shakes. The waking holds sleep off and the quieting puts pain
down; this family answers the tremor, the flinch and the fear, which are none of those
things and spoil more fine work than all of them together. It is the family bought by
people who are well, for the days their work will not allow them to be otherwise.

Nothing in it kills at any dose she could establish. Its counts are small and its buyers
are steady people, which is exactly how its cases arrive: a creature that needs its hands
every day takes the entry that steadies them every day, and a count of 1 climbs like any
other.

Six entries, from the twig chewed at every market to the drop sold four times in her
life:

| Simple      | Form      | Onset      | Duration   | Craving | Price |
| ----------- | --------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------- | ----- |
| Hedgenail   | Chewed    | 1 minute   | 1 hour     | 1       | 2 sp  |
| Steelthread | Chewed    | 1 minute   | 1 hour     | 1       | 5 sp  |
| Doveheart   | Infusion  | 30 minutes | 4 hours    | 1       | 1 gp  |
| Linewort    | Tincture  | 10 minutes | 1 hour     | 2       | 10 gp |
| Greymantle  | Decoction | 30 minutes | 8 hours    | 2       | 12 gp |

### Hedgenail

A hard gray twig-bark stripped from the hedge maple, chewed like drover's cud and doing
for the hands what the cud does for the eyelids. It smells of sap and tastes of green
wood, drying the mouth as it goes.

A measure is a thumb's length of bark, chewed. For 1 hour the creature's hands do not
shake — not from cold, not from fear, not from the morning after, not from want. It
grants nothing else: no Advantage, no courage, only stillness in the fingers, so a
creature whose shaking would spoil a task simply does not shake. There is no overdose;
more of it is more chewing.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 1 to Craving. She sold more of it than of
everything else in this family together, most of it in the first hour of the morning, and
she knew what most of the morning hands wanted it for.

### Steelthread

A pale root like drawn wire, from stony ground, dug small and chewed in the minute before
it is wanted. It smells of iron and earth. It tastes of iron too, and numbs nothing; the
work it does is further in.

A measure is a thumb of root. For 1 hour the creature has Advantage on saving throws
against being Frightened. A second measure in that hour flattens more than fear: the
creature can't take Reactions, nothing arriving fast enough to startle it into one.

**Overdose.** A third measure within the hour: DC 14 Constitution saving throw. On a
failure the creature's Speed is halved and it has Disadvantage on Initiative rolls for 8
hours, everything in it answering slowly.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 1 to Craving. It is a soldier's root, issued
in some companies by the sergeant, and she notes that the sergeants she knew issued it
for one hour and one purpose and kept the pouch themselves.

### Doveheart

A gray-leaved herb with a drooping white flower, of old gardens rather than the wild,
infused. The infusion is pale and smells of the flower. It tastes faintly of almonds and
of nothing much, and it is drunk warm.

A measure is a cup. For 4 hours the creature's body does not do fear's work: the heart
holds, the breath holds, the hands hold, and it has Advantage on saving throws against
being Frightened. A second cup within those hours adds Disadvantage on Wisdom
(Perception) checks; a heart that will not rise does not raise alarms either.

**Overdose.** A third cup within 4 hours: DC 13 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
nothing rouses the creature to speed: for 8 hours it cannot take the Dash action and has
Disadvantage on Initiative rolls.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 1 to Craving.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  Doveheart is bought in the week after a funeral, when the visitors stop coming and the house
  goes quiet. I sell a fortnight of it at a time and no more, and when I am asked why, I say the
  flower is scarce, which is not true. What is scarce is anybody sitting with them, and a fortnight
  is how long the cup takes to become the sitter.
</Voice>

### Linewort

A trailing wort from the feet of old walls, lifted whole and tinctured whole for a month,
and the month in aqua vitae is most of the price. The tincture is green-gold. It smells
of crushed stems and spirit, and three drops taste of nothing at all.

A measure is three drops. For 1 hour the creature has Advantage on ability checks that
turn on a steady hand: Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) checks, work with Thieves' Tools, a
surgeon's Wisdom (Medicine) checks, a fair copy, a stone setting. The hand must be doing
craft. A bow drawn at a living creature is not craft, and the entry is plain that
linewort has never improved one — attack rolls take nothing from it. A second measure
within the hour adds nothing and sours the stomach for the rest of the day.

**Overdose.** A third measure within a day: DC 14 Constitution saving throw. On a failure
the steadiness inverts, and the creature has Disadvantage on Dexterity checks and attack
rolls for 8 hours.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is the trade: a
creature at the third degree does not open the shop, lift the knife or pick the lock
without its drops, and on the other days its hands believe they cannot.

### Greymantle

A black root of standing bog water, decocted for 8 hours to a liquor like strong tea
gone cold. It smells of peat. It tastes of peat and of ash, and it is drunk in one
mouthful because nobody sips it.

A measure is a mouthful. For 8 hours the creature cannot be Frightened and cannot be
Charmed, and it has Disadvantage on Charisma checks and on Wisdom (Insight) checks —
what reads other people was part of what was quieted. It is given to the terrified, and
taken ahead of the things nobody is steady for. A second mouthful within the 8 hours
adds the Poisoned condition for the remainder.

**Overdose.** A third mouthful: DC 15 Constitution saving throw. On a failure the
creature gains 1 Exhaustion level and its Speed is reduced by 10 feet for 8 hours.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 2 to Craving. Its excess is the flat: a
creature at the third degree doses ahead of anything it expects to feel, weddings and
burials alike, and is present at neither.

### Queensmilk

The milk of a small blue flower that grows above the treeline and nowhere below it, a
drop to a flower, gathered in the one month the slopes allow and tinctured. What it costs
is the mountain. The tincture is white as its name. It smells of snow, which is to say of
almost nothing, and it tastes cold and faintly sweet.

A measure is one drop under the tongue, and it acts at once. For 10 minutes the creature
is immune to the Frightened condition; fear it already carries is suspended for the
duration and returns when it ends. A second drop extends nothing. Ten minutes is the
flower's answer, however many drops ask.

**Overdose.** Three or more drops within an hour: DC 15 Constitution saving throw. On a
failure the creature takes 7 (2d6) Necrotic damage as the heart is held too still, and
gains 1 Exhaustion level.

Each day on which a measure was taken adds 3 to Craving. Its excess is the volunteering:
a creature at the third degree seeks out what ought to frighten it, having lost the
feeling of the reason not to, at 3 gp the minute.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I have sold queensmilk four times to men with a date, and I knew the date each time, and I
  charged them nothing, which is between me and my ledger. I have been asked whether that is
  mercy. I have concluded that it is, and I did not conclude it quickly, and I notice the question
  is only ever asked by people who have not stood at the foot of a scaffold with a full bottle in
  their coat.
</Voice>

## Where it is found

This does not work like the drink, and it does not climb. The dangerous end of the catalog
is sold at the bottom of the ladder and at the top, and hardly at all in the middle: a
wretched quarter sells anything to anybody, and a wealthy house has a physician who will
write for it, while a respectable shop in between will not stock what could kill a
customer and be traced. It is the same shape as wormwood water's in the fourth chapter,
across a whole catalog.

Smallweed is outside all of it and is sold everywhere, at every level, like beer. What
follows the house is the grade rather than the leaf: a wretched stall has Rushlight and a
wealthy one has Verrone, and both of them are the same plant.

| Where            | What is to be had                                                       | On what terms                                    |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Wretched**     | Anything, and the hardest of it — sleepwrack, sister's ease, milk poppy | Cut with whatever came to hand, and no questions |
| **Squalid**      | The same, and hushcap, larkwort, quickthorn                             | Cash, no questions, no name given                |
| **Poor**         | Drover's cud, bell-bind, marrowmoss, kindleseed                         | A hedge-witch who gathered it herself            |
| **Modest**       | The same, and emberfoot                                                 | A shop with a name to protect                    |
| **Comfortable**  | The same, and dulcet root, wakerush                                     | An apothecary who asks what for                  |
| **Wealthy**      | Everything, at the measure the entry states                             | A physician who writes it down                   |
| **Aristocratic** | Everything, and nightsmith besides                                      | Somebody else fetches it                         |

The prices in the family tables do not change with the level. What changes is whether
anybody will sell it, how long it takes to arrange, and how many people know afterward.

What is bought below a poor house is not reliably a measure. It may be cut to nothing, or
it may be twice what was asked for, and which of the two it was is discovered after it has
been taken. Everything in this chapter that kills does so at a threshold counted in
measures, so a buyer who does not know what a measure is has lost the only protection the
catalog offers.

<Voice by="Hesper Aldwine">
  I have sold milk poppy to a duchess and to a woman who cleaned for her, at the same price, in the
  same week. The duchess sent a man for it and the woman came herself in the rain, and the
  difference in what it cost the two of them was not in my ledger.
</Voice>
