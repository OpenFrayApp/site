The book arranged for the middle of a session: two lists, one asking what answers a need
and one asking what a coin buys. Each entry's own chapter still holds its measures, its
thresholds and its counts — the lists carry the names and the prices, and nothing here
changes anything there.

Two things carry no price. Thornapple is in every farmyard and no seller's stock, and
the fifth chapter's imitations are sold at the price of whatever each pretends to be,
being worth a few coppers regardless.
